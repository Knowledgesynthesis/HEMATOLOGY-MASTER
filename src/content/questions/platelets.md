:::check peds #itp-mucosal sec=itp-peds refs=ash-itp-2019
A 6-year-old with newly diagnosed ITP (platelets 5 × 10⁹/L) has had epistaxis for 25 minutes that needed packing. She is hemodynamically stable. Which treatment does ASH suggest?
- [ ] Observation only
- [x] Short-course prednisone (2–4 mg/kg/day for 5–7 days); IVIG if a faster rise is needed
- [ ] Platelet transfusion and splenectomy
- [ ] Rituximab
> Non-life-threatening mucosal bleeding is an indication to treat. ASH 2019 suggests a short course of corticosteroids over IVIG/anti-D. IVIG is used when a rapid rise matters.
:::

:::check peds #itp-ich sec=itp-peds refs=ash-itp-2019
A 3-year-old with ITP (platelets 3 × 10⁹/L) falls and develops vomiting and drowsiness. CT shows an intracranial hemorrhage. What is the treatment?
- [ ] IVIG alone, then reassess in 24 h
- [x] Platelet transfusion plus IVIG plus high-dose IV methylprednisolone, together, with neurosurgical input
- [ ] Prednisone 2 mg/kg orally
- [ ] Observation in the ICU
> Life-threatening bleeding needs every rapid-acting modality at once. Platelets can be given repeatedly or as an infusion despite short survival.
:::

:::check adult #itp-2026 sec=itp-adult refs=ash-itp-2026
A 42-year-old with newly diagnosed primary ITP has platelets 11 × 10⁹/L and petechiae only. Under the 2026 ASH guideline, which initial approach is suggested where available?
- [ ] Observation
- [ ] Prednisone alone for 6 weeks
- [x] Corticosteroids combined with rituximab or a thrombopoietic agent (± IVIG)
- [ ] Splenectomy
> The 2026 ASH update suggests steroids plus rituximab or a TPO agent over steroids alone for initial therapy, based on better sustained responses and less rescue therapy. The < 30 × 10⁹/L treatment threshold and ≤ 6-week steroid limit are unchanged.
:::

:::check adult #itp-second sec=itp-adult refs=ash-itp-2026,rilzabrutinib
An adult with ITP relapses after corticosteroids and needs further therapy. What does ASH 2026 recommend first?
- [ ] Splenectomy
- [x] A thrombopoietic agent (eltrombopag, romiplostim or avatrombopag)
- [ ] Danazol
- [ ] Long-term prednisone
> ASH 2026 recommends a TPO agent as second-line therapy and suggests rituximab for those wanting to avoid long-term treatment. For patients who are ineligible or decline, options include a BTK inhibitor (rilzabrutinib, approved 2025), MMF, or fostamatinib.
:::

:::check peds #was sec=inherited refs=nathan-oski
A 10-month-old boy has eczema, recurrent otitis, bloody diarrhea and platelets 22 × 10⁹/L with a low MPV (small platelets). What is the diagnosis?
- [ ] ITP
- [x] Wiskott-Aldrich syndrome
- [ ] Bernard-Soulier syndrome
- [ ] MYH9-related disease
> Microthrombocytopenia, eczema and immunodeficiency in a boy is X-linked WAS. ITP has large platelets. HSCT (or gene therapy) is curative, and lymphoma/autoimmunity risks are high.
:::

:::check both #bss-vs-vwd sec=inherited refs=nathan-oski
A patient has giant platelets, mild thrombocytopenia and absent ristocetin-induced aggregation that does **not** correct with normal plasma. Which disorder is it?
- [ ] VWD type 3
- [x] Bernard-Soulier syndrome
- [ ] Glanzmann thrombasthenia
- [ ] Gray platelet syndrome
> Ristocetin aggregation depends on VWF binding to GPIb. Adding normal plasma (VWF) corrects VWD but not GPIb deficiency (BSS). Glanzmann has normal ristocetin aggregation and absent responses to all other agonists.
:::

:::check peds #reactive-plt sec=thrombocytosis
A 2-year-old recovering from pneumonia has platelets 930 × 10⁹/L, mild microcytic anemia and is otherwise well. What is the management?
- [ ] Start aspirin to prevent thrombosis
- [ ] JAK2 testing and marrow biopsy
- [x] No antiplatelet therapy; recheck after recovery and treat iron deficiency if present
- [ ] Hydroxyurea
> Thrombocytosis in children is almost always reactive (infection, iron deficiency, inflammation) and doesn't cause thrombosis. Treat the cause and recheck.
:::

:::check peds #kmp-q sec=kmp
A 3-month-old has a rapidly enlarging, firm, violaceous thigh mass, platelets 9 × 10⁹/L, fibrinogen 60 mg/dL and high D-dimer. What is the first-line therapy?
- [ ] Propranolol
- [x] Sirolimus (± corticosteroids)
- [ ] Repeated platelet transfusions to > 50 × 10⁹/L
- [ ] Observation; it involutes like infantile hemangioma
> Kasabach-Merritt phenomenon from kaposiform hemangioendothelioma responds to sirolimus. Propranolol is for infantile hemangioma. Platelet transfusion can worsen lesion swelling, so reserve it for bleeding or surgery.
:::
