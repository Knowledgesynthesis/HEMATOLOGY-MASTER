:::check peds #cyclic sec=neutropenia refs=nathan-oski
A 6-year-old has oral ulcers, fever and cervical adenitis roughly every 3 weeks. Between episodes she is well, and a single CBC was normal. What is the best diagnostic test?
- [ ] Bone marrow biopsy now
- [x] CBC with differential 2–3 times weekly for 6 weeks
- [ ] Anti-neutrophil antibodies
- [ ] Repeat one CBC in 6 months
> Cyclic neutropenia (ELANE) causes ~21-day cycles of severe neutropenia. A single CBC can miss the nadir; serial counts document the periodicity. G-CSF reduces symptoms.
:::

:::check peds #sds sec=neutropenia refs=nathan-oski
A 2-year-old with poor weight gain, greasy stools and short stature has ANC 0.7 × 10⁹/L. Sweat chloride is normal. What is the most likely diagnosis?
- [ ] Cystic fibrosis
- [x] Shwachman-Diamond syndrome
- [ ] Autoimmune neutropenia of infancy
- [ ] Celiac disease
> Neutropenia plus exocrine pancreatic insufficiency (low fecal elastase) plus skeletal dysplasia is SDS (SBDS). Monitor for marrow failure and MDS/AML.
:::

:::check peds #fa-before-hsct sec=inherited-bmf refs=nathan-oski
An 8-year-old with short stature and a hypoplastic thumb has severe aplastic anemia. His HLA-identical sister is the proposed donor. What test must be done on both before HSCT?
- [ ] PNH flow cytometry only
- [x] Chromosome breakage testing (DEB/MMC) for Fanconi anemia
- [ ] Hemoglobin electrophoresis
- [ ] Telomere length on the patient only
> Physical anomalies suggest Fanconi anemia, and an affected sibling can be HLA-identical. FA patients also need reduced-intensity conditioning. Test the patient and the sibling donor.
:::

:::check peds #saa-child sec=aplastic-anemia refs=groarke-2022
A 12-year-old has severe aplastic anemia with no inherited syndrome on testing. Her brother is a full HLA match. What is the preferred first-line therapy?
- [ ] Horse ATG + cyclosporine + eltrombopag
- [x] Matched sibling donor HSCT
- [ ] Chronic transfusion only
- [ ] G-CSF and corticosteroids
> Matched sibling HSCT gives excellent long-term survival in children with SAA. Immunosuppression is used without a suitable donor. Adding eltrombopag did not improve response in children.
:::

:::check peds #pertussis sec=lymphocytosis refs=nathan-oski
A 6-week-old has paroxysmal cough, apnea and WBC 58 × 10⁹/L (80% small mature lymphocytes). What does this mean for prognosis and management?
- [ ] Reassuring reactive picture; outpatient care
- [x] High risk of pulmonary hypertension and death; ICU care and consider leukoreduction
- [ ] Diagnostic of ALL; start chemotherapy
- [ ] Indicates EBV; avoid contact sports
> In infant pertussis, marked lymphocytosis (> 30 × 10⁹/L) drives pulmonary hypertension and mortality. Leukoreduction (exchange or leukapheresis) is considered for rising counts or cardiopulmonary compromise.
:::

:::check both #strongy sec=eosinophilia
A 16-year-old who recently immigrated from Southeast Asia has an eosinophil count of 2.1 × 10⁹/L and new asthma-like symptoms. Before starting systemic steroids, what should be done?
- [ ] Nothing; steroids treat eosinophilia
- [x] Test for or empirically treat *Strongyloides* (ivermectin)
- [ ] Start imatinib
- [ ] Bone marrow biopsy
> Steroids can trigger *Strongyloides* hyperinfection, which is often fatal. Serology or empiric ivermectin should come first for anyone with possible exposure.
:::

:::check peds #lad sec=leukocytosis refs=nathan-oski
A 5-week-old's umbilical cord has not separated. He has omphalitis without pus, and WBC is 38 × 10⁹/L (neutrophilia) between infections. What is the likely diagnosis?
- [ ] Severe congenital neutropenia
- [x] Leukocyte adhesion deficiency
- [ ] Chronic granulomatous disease
- [ ] Leukemoid reaction from sepsis only
> LAD (CD18/β2-integrin defects) prevents neutrophil migration into tissues. Expect persistent neutrophilia, delayed cord separation and infections without pus. Confirm with flow cytometry for CD18/CD11.
:::

:::check peds #fn-peds sec=febrile-neutropenia refs=fn-peds-2023
A 7-year-old in ALL induction has T 38.6 °C and ANC 0.1 × 10⁹/L. She is hemodynamically stable. What is the most appropriate empiric therapy, and when?
- [ ] Oral amoxicillin-clavulanate within 4 h
- [x] IV cefepime (or piperacillin-tazobactam) monotherapy within 60 minutes
- [ ] Vancomycin plus meropenem plus amikacin for all
- [ ] Wait for blood culture results
> The 2023 pediatric guideline recommends empiric antipseudomonal β-lactam monotherapy within 1 hour. Add other agents only for instability, resistance concerns or specific indications.
:::
