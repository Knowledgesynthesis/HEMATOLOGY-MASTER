:::check peds #fava sec=g6pd refs=who-g6pd-2022
A 5-year-old boy of Mediterranean ancestry develops pallor and cola-colored urine 2 days after eating fava beans. Hb 6.4 g/dL, DAT negative, bite cells on smear. What is the most likely diagnosis?
- [ ] Hereditary spherocytosis
- [x] G6PD deficiency
- [ ] Paroxysmal cold hemoglobinuria
- [ ] Warm AIHA
> Oxidant-triggered acute intravascular hemolysis with bite/blister cells and a negative DAT in a boy is G6PD deficiency. Confirm enzyme activity after recovery, and give the family a list of triggers and drugs to avoid.
:::

:::check peds #evans sec=aiha refs=aiha-consensus-2020
A 9-year-old has warm AIHA (DAT IgG+) and ITP. Lymph nodes are enlarged and the spleen is palpable. What additional evaluation matters most?
- [ ] None; treat with steroids alone
- [x] Immune dysregulation work-up (double-negative T cells for ALPS, immunoglobulins, genetic panel)
- [ ] Hemoglobin electrophoresis
- [ ] Osmotic gradient ektacytometry
> Pediatric Evans syndrome is often a manifestation of an inborn error of immunity (ALPS, CTLA4, LRBA, CVID). Diagnosis changes therapy (e.g., sirolimus, abatacept).
:::

:::check adult #dapsone-mb sec=g6pd refs=who-g6pd-2022
A man on dapsone is found to have methemoglobinemia (MetHb 28%) and hemolysis. He is G6PD-deficient. Which therapy should be avoided?
- [ ] Stopping dapsone
- [ ] Supplemental oxygen
- [x] Methylene blue
- [ ] Red cell exchange for severe cases
> Methylene blue needs NADPH (from G6PD) to work. In G6PD deficiency it can worsen hemolysis and fails to reduce MetHb. Alternatives include ascorbic acid and exchange transfusion in severe cases.
:::

:::check adult #ttp-now sec=ttp refs=isth-ttp-2020,plasmic,hercules
A 34-year-old woman is confused. Platelets 9 × 10⁹/L, schistocytes, LDH 1,450 U/L, creatinine 1.1 mg/dL, INR 1.1, MCV 88 fL, no cancer or transplant. ADAMTS13 results will take 2 days. What do you do now?
- [ ] Wait for ADAMTS13 before plasma exchange
- [ ] Transfuse platelets to > 50 × 10⁹/L
- [x] Start plasma exchange, corticosteroids and caplacizumab now
- [ ] Give eculizumab
> PLASMIC score 7 (high probability). Start iTTP treatment immediately while awaiting ADAMTS13. Avoid platelet transfusion unless there is life-threatening bleeding.
:::

:::check peds #pneumo-hus sec=hus refs=nathan-oski
A 14-month-old with pneumococcal empyema develops anemia with schistocytes, thrombocytopenia and AKI. The DAT is positive. Which blood product practice is recommended?
- [ ] Plasma infusion to replace complement
- [x] Avoid plasma; use washed or low-plasma products per blood bank
- [ ] Platelet transfusion to > 50 × 10⁹/L
- [ ] IVIG 2 g/kg
> In pneumococcal HUS, neuraminidase exposes the T-antigen. Plasma contains anti-T antibodies that may worsen hemolysis. Supportive care and dialysis are the mainstays.
:::

:::check peds #cblc sec=hus refs=nathan-oski
A 3-month-old has TMA (schistocytes, thrombocytopenia, AKI), poor feeding and hypotonia. No diarrhea. Which test is most important to send?
- [ ] Stool Shiga toxin
- [x] Plasma total homocysteine and methylmalonic acid
- [ ] Anti-factor H antibodies
- [ ] Hemoglobin electrophoresis
> Cobalamin C (cblC) disease causes infantile TMA with very high homocysteine and MMA. It is treatable with parenteral hydroxocobalamin. Send these before complement work-up in infants.
:::

:::check adult #pnh-q sec=pnh refs=nathan-oski
A 29-year-old has DAT-negative hemolysis, pancytopenia and hepatic vein thrombosis (Budd-Chiari). Which test is diagnostic?
- [ ] Osmotic fragility
- [x] Peripheral blood flow cytometry for GPI-anchored proteins (FLAER, CD59)
- [ ] JAK2 V617F only
- [ ] Ham test
> Hemolysis, marrow failure and unusual-site thrombosis is the PNH triad. High-sensitivity flow cytometry is the standard (the Ham test is obsolete). JAK2 is worth checking in Budd-Chiari but doesn't explain hemolysis.
:::
