:::check peds #early-vkdb sec=vkdb refs=aap-vitk-2022
A newborn develops a large cephalohematoma and GI bleeding at 8 hours of life. The mother takes phenytoin for epilepsy. INR 6.0, platelets normal. What is the diagnosis?
- [ ] Classic VKDB
- [x] Early VKDB from maternal enzyme-inducing drugs
- [ ] Hemophilia A
- [ ] DIC from sepsis
> Early VKDB (< 24 h) occurs with maternal anticonvulsants (phenytoin, phenobarbital, carbamazepine), rifampin, isoniazid or warfarin. Give IV vitamin K plus PCC or plasma for active bleeding. Mothers on these drugs may receive vitamin K in late pregnancy.
:::

:::check peds #polycythemia-q sec=polycythemia refs=nathan-oski
A 4-hour-old infant of a diabetic mother is jittery with glucose 32 mg/dL. Capillary Hct is 74%. What is the most important next step?
- [ ] Immediate partial exchange transfusion
- [x] Treat hypoglycemia and confirm Hct with a venous sample
- [ ] Phlebotomy of 10 mL/kg
- [ ] Start aspirin
> Capillary Hct overestimates venous Hct. Treat hypoglycemia first, then measure venous Hct. Partial exchange is reserved for symptomatic infants with confirmed venous Hct > 65%, and evidence for long-term benefit is weak.
:::

:::check peds #anti-kell sec=hdfn refs=bsh-neonatal-2016
A newborn has Hb 7.2 g/dL with only mild hyperbilirubinemia and retic 1.1%. The mother has a known antibody from a prior transfusion. Which antibody fits best?
- [ ] Anti-A
- [ ] Anti-D
- [x] Anti-K (Kell)
- [ ] Anti-Lea
> Anti-Kell destroys erythroid progenitors as well as mature cells. The result is anemia out of proportion to bilirubin, with inappropriately low reticulocytes. Kell antibody titers also predict fetal anemia poorly, so MCA Doppler is used.
:::

:::check peds #late-hdfn sec=hdfn refs=bsh-neonatal-2016
A 5-week-old who had intrauterine transfusions for anti-D HDFN now has Hb 6.8 g/dL and retic 0.5%. Bilirubin is normal. What is the explanation?
- [ ] Iron deficiency
- [x] Late hyporegenerative anemia of HDFN
- [ ] Transient erythroblastopenia of childhood
- [ ] Diamond-Blackfan anemia
> Persisting maternal antibody plus suppressed erythropoiesis (after IUT and transfused HbA) causes late anemia at weeks 2–12. Monitor Hb weekly. Top-up transfusion is often needed; some centers use EPO.
:::

:::check peds #tam-q sec=down-syndrome refs=who-haem5-myeloid
A 2-day-old with trisomy 21 is well and feeding. WBC 42 × 10⁹/L with 28% blasts; mild hepatomegaly. What is the best management?
- [ ] Induction chemotherapy for AML
- [x] Observation with serial CBCs; TAM usually resolves by 3 months
- [ ] Urgent bone marrow transplant
- [ ] Leukapheresis
> Transient abnormal myelopoiesis (GATA1-mutated) usually resolves spontaneously. Treat only life-threatening features (hyperviscosity, liver failure, hydrops) with low-dose cytarabine. Follow for ML-DS through age 4.
:::

:::check peds #pih-plt sec=neonatal-thrombocytopenia refs=nathan-oski
A growth-restricted preterm infant of a mother with preeclampsia has platelets 85 × 10⁹/L, ANC 1.2 and many nucleated RBCs on day 1. She is otherwise stable. What is the likely course?
- [ ] FNAIT requiring HPA-matched platelets
- [x] Placental insufficiency–related thrombocytopenia; resolves over 7–10 days
- [ ] Congenital amegakaryocytic thrombocytopenia
- [ ] Early-onset sepsis requiring platelet transfusion
> Mild–moderate early thrombocytopenia with neutropenia and nRBCs is typical of placental insufficiency (chronic fetal hypoxia). No treatment is needed beyond monitoring. Reconsider if counts fall below 50 × 10⁹/L or the baby becomes ill.
:::

:::check peds #pf-neonate sec=neonatal-thrombosis refs=ash-peds-vte-2025
At 18 hours old, a term infant develops rapidly spreading purpuric skin lesions, then DIC. There is no infection. Protein C activity is undetectable. What is the immediate treatment?
- [ ] Platelet transfusion
- [x] Fresh frozen plasma or protein C concentrate
- [ ] Vitamin K
- [ ] Recombinant FVIIa
> Neonatal purpura fulminans from homozygous protein C deficiency needs immediate replacement: protein C concentrate if available, otherwise FFP 10–20 mL/kg every 6–12 h. Long-term anticoagulation and/or replacement follow.
:::
