:::check peds #iron-failure sec=ida-peds refs=aap-iron-2026,powers-2017
After 4 weeks of ferrous sulfate 3 mg/kg/day, a 2-year-old's Hb is unchanged. The parent mixes the iron into the toddler's milk bottle. What is the most likely reason for failure?
- [ ] IRIDA (TMPRSS6 mutation)
- [x] Impaired absorption from co-administration with milk
- [ ] Undiagnosed β-thalassemia trait
- [ ] Celiac disease
> Calcium and casein in milk inhibit non-heme iron absorption. Give iron on an empty stomach (water or juice), keep restricting milk, and check adherence before looking for rarer causes.

- [ ] Oral iron and recheck in 3 months; no further testing
- [x] Bidirectional endoscopy (EGD and colonoscopy)
- [ ] Capsule endoscopy first
- [ ] Hemoglobin electrophoresis
> Iron deficiency in men and postmenopausal women is GI blood loss (including malignancy) until proven otherwise (AGA 2020). Bidirectional endoscopy plus non-invasive celiac and *H. pylori* testing come first.

- [x] Oral succimer chelation, environmental investigation, and iron repletion if deficient
- [ ] Admission for IV CaNa₂EDTA and dimercaprol

> BLL ≥ 45 µg/dL warrants chelation (oral succimer) once the child is in a lead-safe environment. Admission and IV therapy are for ≥ 70 µg/dL or encephalopathy.

:::check peds #infant-b12 sec=macrocytic refs=nathan-oski
A 9-month-old exclusively breastfed by a vegan mother is losing milestones and is hypotonic. Hb 7.9 g/dL, MCV 108 fL, hypersegmented neutrophils. What is the best management?
- [ ] Folic acid 1 mg/day
- [ ] MRI and EEG; no hematologic treatment yet
- [x] Intramuscular B12 for the infant and evaluation and treatment of the mother
- [ ] Iron supplementation
> Nutritional B12 deficiency in infants of B12-deficient mothers causes regression and hypotonia. Early IM B12 can reverse much of the damage. Folate alone could worsen neurologic injury.

An adolescent with active Crohn disease has Hb 9.8 g/dL, MCV 77 fL, ferritin 65 ng/mL, TSAT 8% and CRP 45 mg/L. What is the interpretation and plan?
- [ ] Anemia of inflammation only; treat the Crohn disease
- [x] Combined iron deficiency and inflammation; IV iron is preferred
- [ ] Thalassemia trait
- [ ] Iron overload
> With inflammation, ferritin up to ~100 (WHO < 70 µg/L for ≥ 5 years) and TSAT < 20% indicate iron deficiency. Active IBD reduces oral iron absorption and tolerance, so IV iron is usually preferred.

:::check adult #copper sec=sideroblastic refs=who-haem5-myeloid
A 52-year-old with prior gastric bypass takes high-dose zinc supplements. She has anemia, neutropenia and a sensory ataxia; B12 is normal. Marrow shows ring sideroblasts and vacuolated precursors. What is the cause?
- [ ] MDS with SF3B1 mutation
- [x] Copper deficiency
- [ ] Lead poisoning
- [ ] Folate deficiency
> Zinc excess and bariatric surgery cause copper deficiency. It mimics MDS (ring sideroblasts, vacuoles, cytopenias) and B12 myeloneuropathy. Stopping zinc and replacing copper reverses the cytopenias.
:::

:::check peds #preterm-iron sec=ida-peds refs=aap-iron-2026
How should iron be supplemented in a healthy breastfed infant born at 32 weeks?
- [ ] No supplementation until 12 months
- [x] Elemental iron 2–3 mg/kg/day starting in the first weeks of life, through 12 months
- [ ] 1 mg/kg/day starting at 6 months

A 6-year-old with IDA took ferrous sulfate 3 mg/kg once daily correctly (confirmed) for 4 weeks. Hb rose only 0.2 g/dL. Diet is adequate and milk intake is modest. What is the best next step?

- [x] Look for celiac disease, *H. pylori*, ongoing GI or other blood loss, and reconsider the diagnosis
