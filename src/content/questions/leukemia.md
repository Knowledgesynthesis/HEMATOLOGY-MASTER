A 4-year-old with new B-ALL has WBC 12 × 10⁹/L, ETV6::RUNX1 and no CNS disease. End-of-induction MRD is negative. Which statement is correct?

- [ ] Should receive a TKI

- [ ] CYP2D6
- [x] TPMT and NUDT15 genotyping
- [ ] DPYD
- [ ] UGT1A1

:::check both #hyperleuko sec=emergencies
A 13-year-old with new AML has WBC 210 × 10⁹/L, Hb 6.1 g/dL, headache and SpO₂ 91%. What should be avoided while arranging cytoreduction?
- [ ] Hydroxyurea
- [ ] Platelet transfusion for counts < 20 × 10⁹/L
- [x] Rapid large-volume red cell transfusion
- [ ] Allopurinol and hydration
> Leukostasis worsens with higher whole-blood viscosity. Defer or minimize RBC transfusion (small volumes slowly if essential) until the WBC falls. Platelets and plasma don't add much viscosity.
:::

:::check peds #mediastinal sec=emergencies
A 15-year-old has orthopnea, a large anterior mediastinal mass and a pleural effusion. WBC is normal. Which diagnostic approach is safest?
- [ ] CT-guided mass biopsy under general anesthesia
- [x] Diagnose from pleural fluid or peripheral sites (flow cytometry) under local anesthesia, avoiding general anesthesia
- [ ] Start steroids, then biopsy in 1 week
- [ ] Mediastinoscopy under deep sedation
> Supine positioning and anesthesia can cause airway or cardiovascular collapse. Get tissue or cells by the least invasive route (pleural fluid, peripheral node, blood or marrow). Give empiric steroids only if the patient is unstable.
:::

:::check both #cml-failure sec=cml refs=eln-cml-2020
At 12 months of imatinib for chronic-phase CML, BCR::ABL1 (IS) is 2.5%. How does ELN 2020 classify this, and what is the action?
- [ ] Optimal; continue
- [ ] Warning; recheck in 6 months
- [x] Failure; check adherence and ABL1 kinase mutations, then switch TKI
- [ ] Treatment-free remission candidate
> At 12 months, > 1% is failure (optimal ≤ 0.1%, warning 0.1–1%). Assess adherence and drug interactions, test for kinase-domain mutations, and switch to a second-generation TKI or asciminib.

:::check peds #jmml sec=mds-jmml refs=who-haem5-myeloid
A 2-year-old with multiple café-au-lait macules has massive splenomegaly, WBC 38 × 10⁹/L with monocytes 6 × 10⁹/L, platelets 40 × 10⁹/L, raised HbF for age and 4% blasts. What is the diagnosis?
- [ ] CML
- [x] Juvenile myelomonocytic leukemia (NF1-associated)
- [ ] Infectious mononucleosis
- [ ] AML
> Monocytosis ≥ 1 × 10⁹/L, splenomegaly, ↑HbF and < 20% blasts in a young child with NF1 is JMML (RAS pathway). Confirm BCR::ABL1 is negative. HSCT is curative.

:::check both #diff-syndrome sec=apl refs=apl0406
On day 6 of ATRA + arsenic for APL, a patient develops fever, dyspnea, bilateral infiltrates, a 4-kg weight gain and rising creatinine. What is the treatment?
- [ ] Add broad-spectrum antibiotics only
- [x] Dexamethasone immediately (pause ATRA/ATO if severe), plus supportive care
- [ ] Increase the ATRA dose
- [ ] Platelet transfusion
> This is differentiation syndrome. Dexamethasone (adults 10 mg IV q12h) at the first signs reduces mortality. Hold differentiating agents temporarily if severe, and treat infection in parallel if suspected.

:::check peds #asparaginase-pancreatitis sec=all-peds
During ALL therapy after pegaspargase, a 12-year-old develops severe epigastric pain radiating to the back. Lipase is 1,400 U/L. What is the most likely cause?
- [ ] Steroid-induced gastritis
- [x] Asparaginase-associated pancreatitis
- [ ] Vincristine ileus
- [ ] Methotrexate hepatotoxicity
> Pancreatitis is a classic asparaginase toxicity, along with thrombosis (including CSVT), hypersensitivity, hyperglycemia and liver injury. Severe pancreatitis usually ends further asparaginase.

:::check peds #aml-clues sec=aml-peds
Which presentation favors AML over ALL in a teenager with new pancytopenia and circulating blasts?
- [ ] Mediastinal mass
- [x] Gingival hypertrophy and DIC
- [ ] Bone pain
- [ ] TdT-positive blasts
> Gingival or skin infiltration (monocytic subtypes), chloromas and coagulopathy (APL, monocytic) point to AML. Mediastinal masses and TdT positivity favor lymphoblastic disease.

- [ ] Annual bone marrow
- [x] Linear growth (height velocity) and pubertal development
- [ ] Monthly echocardiogram
- [ ] Weekly CBC indefinitely
> TKIs inhibit growth-plate signaling, and prepubertal children can lose height potential. Discuss dosing strategies and treatment-free remission eligibility with pediatric CML experts.

:::check peds #tls-phos sec=emergencies
Which phosphate level meets the Cairo-Bishop laboratory TLS criterion in children?
- [ ] ≥ 4.5 mg/dL
- [x] ≥ 6.5 mg/dL
- [ ] ≥ 3.0 mg/dL
- [ ] ≥ 10 mg/dL
> Children have higher normal phosphate, so the threshold is ≥ 6.5 mg/dL (≥ 4.5 in adults). Lab TLS needs ≥ 2 of: uric acid ≥ 8, K ≥ 6, phosphate threshold, Ca ≤ 7 mg/dL.
