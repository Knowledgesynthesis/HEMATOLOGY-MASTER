:::check peds #nadir sec=normal-values refs=harriet-lane
A thriving 7-week-old born at term has Hb 9.6 g/dL, MCV 92 fL, retic 1.5%, and normal WBC and platelets. What is the next step?
- [ ] Iron supplementation 3 mg/kg/day
- [ ] Hemoglobin electrophoresis
- [x] Reassurance: physiologic nadir
- [ ] Transfuse 10 mL/kg PRBC
> At 2 months the lower limit of Hb is ~9.0 g/dL. EPO switches off after birth and neonatal RBCs have shorter survival, so Hb nadirs at 8–12 weeks before recovering. Term iron stores are adequate until ~4–6 months.
:::

:::check peds #hbf-switch sec=hb-switching refs=nathan-oski
Why do children with HbSS usually become symptomatic only after ~6 months of age?
- [ ] Maternal antibodies protect against sickling
- [x] γ-globin (HbF) production falls as β-globin (HbS) production rises
- [ ] The spleen does not function until 6 months
- [ ] HbS is not produced until after birth
> HbF (α₂γ₂) dominates at birth and inhibits HbS polymerization. As the γ→β switch completes over the first year, HbS dominates and vaso-occlusion begins. Dactylitis at 6–24 months is a classic first sign.
:::

:::check both #arc sec=retic refs=nathan-oski
Retic 4% and RBC count 2.5 × 10¹²/L. What is the absolute reticulocyte count?
- [ ] 10 × 10⁹/L
- [ ] 40 × 10⁹/L
- [x] 100 × 10⁹/L
- [ ] 250 × 10⁹/L
> ARC = 0.04 × 2.5 × 10¹² = 100 × 10⁹/L. In an anemic patient, an ARC > 100 × 10⁹/L indicates an appropriate marrow response.
:::

:::check both #cold-artifact sec=cbc refs=aiha-consensus-2020
A 19-year-old with *Mycoplasma* pneumonia has a CBC showing MCV 118 fL, MCHC 41 g/dL and a low RBC count. Hb by co-oximetry is near normal. What is the best next step?
- [ ] Bone marrow biopsy for MDS
- [ ] Start B12 and folate
- [x] Warm the sample to 37 °C and repeat the CBC
- [ ] Transfuse for severe anemia
> Cold agglutinins clump RBCs at room temperature. Doublets are counted as large single cells, so MCV and MCHC rise falsely and RBC count falls. Warming the sample corrects the indices.
:::

:::check both #pseudo-plt sec=cbc refs=nathan-oski
An asymptomatic adult has platelets 38 × 10⁹/L on routine labs; last year's count was normal. Exam shows no bleeding, and the smear shows large platelet aggregates. What is the next step?
- [ ] Start prednisone for ITP
- [x] Repeat the count in a citrate (or heparin) tube
- [ ] Bone marrow examination
- [ ] Platelet transfusion before any procedure
> EDTA-dependent pseudothrombocytopenia (~0.1% of people) causes in-vitro clumping. Confirm with a citrate tube and smear review before any work-up or treatment.
:::
