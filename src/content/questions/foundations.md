A thriving 7-week-old born at term has Hb 9.6 g/dL, MCV 92 fL, retic 1.5%, and normal WBC and platelets. What is the next step?
- [ ] Iron supplementation 3 mg/kg/day
- [ ] Hemoglobin electrophoresis
- [x] Reassurance: physiologic nadir
- [ ] Transfuse 10 mL/kg PRBC
> At 2 months the lower limit of Hb is ~9.0 g/dL. EPO switches off after birth and neonatal RBCs have shorter survival, so Hb nadirs at 8–12 weeks before recovering. Term iron stores are adequate until ~4–6 months.
:::

:::check peds #hbf-switch sec=hb-switching refs=nathan-oski
Why do children with HbSS usually become symptomatic only after ~6 months of age?
- [ ] Maternal antibodies protect against sickling
- [x] γ-globin (HbF) production falls as β-globin (HbS) production rises
- [ ] The spleen does not function until 6 months
- [ ] HbS is not produced until after birth
> HbF (α₂γ₂) dominates at birth and inhibits HbS polymerization. As the γ→β switch completes over the first year, HbS dominates and vaso-occlusion begins. Dactylitis at 6–24 months is a classic first sign.
:::

:::check both #arc sec=retic refs=nathan-oski
Retic 4% and RBC count 2.5 × 10¹²/L. What is the absolute reticulocyte count?
- [ ] 10 × 10⁹/L
- [ ] 40 × 10⁹/L
- [x] 100 × 10⁹/L
- [ ] 250 × 10⁹/L
> ARC = 0.04 × 2.5 × 10¹² = 100 × 10⁹/L. In an anemic patient, an ARC > 100 × 10⁹/L indicates an appropriate marrow response.
:::

:::check both #cold-artifact sec=cbc refs=aiha-consensus-2020
A 19-year-old with *Mycoplasma* pneumonia has a CBC showing MCV 118 fL, MCHC 41 g/dL and a low RBC count. Hb by co-oximetry is near normal. What is the best next step?
- [ ] Bone marrow biopsy for MDS
- [ ] Start B12 and folate
- [x] Warm the sample to 37 °C and repeat the CBC
- [ ] Transfuse for severe anemia
> Cold agglutinins clump RBCs at room temperature. Doublets are counted as large single cells, so MCV and MCHC rise falsely and RBC count falls. Warming the sample corrects the indices.
:::

:::check both #pseudo-plt sec=cbc refs=nathan-oski
An asymptomatic adult has platelets 38 × 10⁹/L on routine labs; last year's count was normal. Exam shows no bleeding, and the smear shows large platelet aggregates. What is the next step?
- [ ] Start prednisone for ITP
- [x] Repeat the count in a citrate (or heparin) tube
- [ ] Bone marrow examination
- [ ] Platelet transfusion before any procedure
> EDTA-dependent pseudothrombocytopenia (~0.1% of people) causes in-vitro clumping. Confirm with a citrate tube and smear review before any work-up or treatment.
:::

:::check peds #newborn-retic sec=retic refs=nathan-oski
A healthy term newborn, 18 hours old, has retic 4.8% and Hb 18.2 g/dL. How do you interpret this?
- [ ] Hemolysis: send a DAT and bilirubin now
- [x] Normal for the first days of life
- [ ] Polycythemia needing partial exchange
- [ ] Marrow stress from occult blood loss
> Retic is 3–7% at birth and falls below 1% within 1–2 weeks as EPO switches off. Hb 18.2 is within the 1–3-day range (mean 18.5, lower limit 14.5).
:::

:::check peds #lymph-predominance sec=normal-values refs=harriet-lane
A healthy 2-year-old's routine CBC shows WBC 10.2 × 10⁹/L with lymphocytes 7.5 and neutrophils 2.0. How do you interpret this?
- [ ] Lymphocytosis: send flow cytometry for leukemia
- [ ] Neutropenia: repeat in 1 week
- [x] Normal lymphocyte-predominant differential for age
- [ ] Viral infection; check EBV serology
> Lymphocytes normally outnumber neutrophils from ~1 week to ~4–6 years. At this age the normal ALC is 3–9.5 and ANC is ≥ 1.5.
:::

:::check peds #mcv-6y sec=cbc refs=harriet-lane
A 6-year-old has Hb 11.9 g/dL and MCV 73 fL. Using age-specific norms, how is the MCV classified?
- [ ] Normal (lower limit 70 fL at all pediatric ages)
- [x] Microcytic (lower limit ≈ 76–77 fL at 6 years)
- [ ] Macrocytic
- [ ] Can't be classified without an RBC count
> Rule of thumb: lower limit of MCV ≈ 70 + age in years. At 6, that is ~76 (table value 77 for 6–12 years). Work up the microcytosis: ferritin, then HPLC if ferritin is normal.
:::

:::check peds #neonatal-coags sec=hemostasis refs=andrew-dev-hemostasis
Before a circumcision, a healthy term 2-day-old has a PT slightly above the adult reference range. The aPTT is within the neonatal range and there is no bleeding history. What should you do?
- [ ] Cancel and give FFP 10 mL/kg
- [ ] Work up for hemophilia
- [x] Interpret against age-specific neonatal ranges; mildly prolonged values are physiologic
- [ ] Give platelet transfusion
> Vitamin K–dependent and contact factors are ~30–60% of adult levels at birth, so neonatal PT/aPTT run longer. Use gestational age–specific ranges and confirm vitamin K was given.
:::

:::check peds #chr sec=retic refs=nathan-oski
Which test reflects iron-restricted erythropoiesis within days, before the Hb or MCV falls, and is useful in infants?
- [ ] Serum ferritin
- [ ] Hemoglobin A₂
- [x] Reticulocyte hemoglobin content (CHr / Ret-He)
- [ ] RDW
> Reticulocytes live 1–2 days in blood, so their Hb content shows current iron availability. Ferritin rises with inflammation, and the MCV changes slowly.
:::
