A 16-year-old with SLE has a DVT and persistently triple-positive antiphospholipid antibodies. Which anticoagulant is preferred long-term?
- [ ] Rivaroxaban
- [ ] Dabigatran
- [x] Warfarin (target INR 2–3), with LMWH bridging initially
- [ ] Aspirin alone
> APS is a setting where DOACs should not be used or used with great caution (ASH/ISTH 2025). Rivaroxaban caused excess arterial events in triple-positive adults (TRAPS). Use a VKA.

:::check peds #rvt-bilateral mod=neonatal sec=neonatal-thrombosis refs=ash-peds-vte-2025
A 3-day-old has bilateral renal vein thrombosis extending into the IVC, with oliguric renal failure. Which approach do the 2025 guidelines suggest?
- [ ] Observation
- [ ] Anticoagulation alone
- [x] Thrombolysis followed by anticoagulation
- [ ] Platelet transfusion only
> Life-threatening neonatal RVT is the case where thrombolysis followed by anticoagulation is suggested. Non-life-threatening RVT is treated with anticoagulation alone.

:::check peds #svt-cannula sec=peds-vte-treat refs=ash-peds-vte-2025
A 9-year-old has a tender, cord-like superficial vein in the forearm at the site of a recent peripheral IV. No DVT on ultrasound. What is recommended?
- [ ] LMWH for 6 weeks
- [ ] A DOAC for 3 months
- [x] No anticoagulation; remove the cannula and give symptomatic care
- [ ] Thrombolysis
> Cannula-related superficial vein thrombosis of the upper limb doesn't need anticoagulation (ASH/ISTH 2025). Non-cannula or lower-limb superficial thrombosis with risk factors is treated.
:::

:::check adult #unprovoked-pe sec=adult-vte refs=ash-vte-2020
A 48-year-old completes 6 months of apixaban for an unprovoked PE. Bleeding risk is low. What do ASH 2020 guidelines suggest?
- [ ] Stop anticoagulation
- [x] Continue indefinitely, often with reduced-dose apixaban
- [ ] Switch to aspirin only
- [ ] Place an IVC filter and stop anticoagulation
> After unprovoked VTE, recurrence risk after stopping is high (~10% in year 1). Indefinite therapy is suggested if bleeding risk allows. Reduced-dose DOACs are effective for extended prevention with low bleeding risk.
:::

:::check both #thrombophilia-timing sec=thrombophilia refs=ash-thrombophilia-2023
A patient with an acute DVT, on heparin for 2 days, has a "thrombophilia panel" ordered. Which result can be interpreted reliably now?
- [ ] Antithrombin activity
- [ ] Protein C activity
- [ ] Protein S activity
- [x] Factor V Leiden genotype
> Acute thrombosis and anticoagulants alter antithrombin, protein C and protein S levels; heparin lowers AT. DNA tests (FVL, prothrombin G20210A) are unaffected. Most panels sent during acute VTE shouldn't be sent at all.
:::

:::check peds #contraception-vte sec=estrogen refs=us-mec-2024
A 17-year-old with a prior estrogen-associated DVT, now off anticoagulation, requests contraception. Which is most appropriate?
- [ ] Combined oral contraceptive pill
- [ ] Contraceptive vaginal ring
- [x] Levonorgestrel IUD (or etonogestrel implant)
- [ ] Transdermal estrogen-progestin patch
> Estrogen is category 4 after VTE with higher recurrence risk (US MEC 2024). Progestin-only long-acting methods are safe and reduce heavy menstrual bleeding.
:::

:::check adult #warfarin-ich sec=reversal refs=annexa-i
A patient on warfarin (INR 3.8) presents with intracerebral hemorrhage. What is the reversal of choice?
- [ ] Fresh frozen plasma 2 units
- [ ] Vitamin K orally
- [x] IV vitamin K plus 4-factor prothrombin complex concentrate
- [ ] Protamine sulfate
> 4F-PCC normalizes the INR within minutes with less volume than plasma. IV vitamin K provides sustained reversal. Plasma is a fallback only if PCC is unavailable.
:::

:::check adult #dabi-rev sec=reversal
A patient on dabigatran needs emergency surgery for a perforated viscus. Which agent specifically reverses dabigatran?
- [ ] Andexanet alfa
- [x] Idarucizumab
- [ ] Protamine
- [ ] Vitamin K
> Idarucizumab (5 g IV) is a monoclonal antibody fragment that binds dabigatran. Andexanet reverses factor Xa inhibitors. Dabigatran is also dialyzable.
:::

:::check peds #cvad-risk sec=peds-vte-epi refs=ash-peds-vte-2025
What is the most important single risk factor for venous thromboembolism in hospitalized children?
- [ ] Factor V Leiden
- [x] A central venous access device
- [ ] Obesity
- [ ] Recent vaccination
> CVADs underlie most neonatal and a large share of childhood VTE. Minimizing line duration and promptly removing lines no longer needed are key prevention steps.
:::

:::check peds #enox-dose sec=peds-vte-treat refs=ash-peds-vte-2018
A 9-month-old (8 kg) needs enoxaparin for a DVT. What is the treatment dose and monitoring?
- [ ] 0.5 mg/kg once daily; no monitoring
- [x] 1 mg/kg every 12 h (8 mg q12h), with anti-Xa 0.5–1.0 U/mL measured 4–6 h after a dose
- [ ] 1.5 mg/kg every 12 h; monitor the aPTT
- [ ] 40 mg daily
> Infants ≥ 2 months: 1 mg/kg q12h. Under 2 months: ~1.5 mg/kg q12h (neonates often need more). Titrate to anti-Xa, because pharmacokinetics vary with age.
:::

:::check peds #csvt-hemorrhage sec=peds-vte-treat refs=ash-peds-vte-2025
A 10-year-old with mastoiditis has transverse sinus thrombosis with a small venous hemorrhagic infarct. What do ASH/ISTH 2025 suggest?
- [ ] Withhold anticoagulation because of the hemorrhage
- [x] Anticoagulation (no thrombolysis), with treatment of the infection
- [ ] Thrombolysis followed by anticoagulation
- [ ] Aspirin only
> CSVT with or without hemorrhage from venous congestion: anticoagulation is suggested, and anticoagulation alone is preferred over thrombolysis. Repeat imaging guides duration.
:::

:::check peds #nephrotic sec=peds-vte-epi
Why are children with nephrotic syndrome at increased risk of venous thrombosis?
- [ ] Thrombocytopenia
- [x] Urinary loss of antithrombin plus increased hepatic synthesis of procoagulants, hemoconcentration and immobility
- [ ] Low fibrinogen
- [ ] Vitamin K deficiency
> Antithrombin loss also causes heparin resistance. Renal vein thrombosis and CSVT are characteristic sites.
:::

:::check peds #unprovoked-teen sec=peds-vte-treat refs=ash-peds-vte-2025
A 16-year-old has an unprovoked proximal DVT with no ongoing risk factors and no known thrombophilia. What duration do ASH/ISTH 2025 suggest?
- [ ] 6 weeks
- [ ] 3 months
- [x] 6–12 months
- [ ] Indefinite
> Unprovoked pediatric DVT/PE: 6–12 months is suggested over indefinite anticoagulation. Provoked VTE: 3 months, or 6 weeks if low-risk.
:::

:::check peds #cvad-thrombophilia sec=thrombophilia refs=ash-thrombophilia-2023
A 2-year-old develops a CVAD-related upper-extremity DVT during chemotherapy. The family asks about a "clotting panel". What do you advise?
- [ ] Full thrombophilia panel now to guide anticoagulation
- [x] Testing isn't indicated: results wouldn't change management of a catheter-provoked clot (and acute illness or anticoagulants distort several assays)
- [ ] Protein C and S levels only
- [ ] Test all siblings
> Inherited thrombophilia rarely changes the treatment of provoked pediatric VTE. Selective testing is for purpura fulminans, neonatal VTE without a line, unprovoked VTE, or strong family history.
:::
