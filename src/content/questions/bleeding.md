:::check peds #f13 sec=rare-factors refs=wfh-2020
A 10-day-old had prolonged umbilical stump bleeding, and now a head ultrasound shows a small ICH after an uneventful delivery. PT, aPTT, fibrinogen and platelets are normal. Which deficiency is most likely?
- [ ] Factor VIII
- [ ] Factor VII
- [x] Factor XIII
- [ ] von Willebrand factor
> FXIII deficiency doesn't prolong PT or aPTT. Umbilical bleeding and ICH are hallmark presentations. Order an FXIII activity assay. Monthly prophylaxis with FXIII concentrate prevents ICH.

:::check peds #emi-bleed sec=hemophilia refs=haven-1,wfh-2020
A 6-year-old with severe hemophilia A without inhibitors, on emicizumab prophylaxis, has an acute ankle hemarthrosis. His aPTT is normal. What is the best treatment?
- [ ] No treatment; the normal aPTT shows adequate coverage
- [x] Factor VIII concentrate dosed for a joint bleed
- [ ] aPCC 100 U/kg every 8 hours
- [ ] Cryoprecipitate
> Emicizumab normalizes the aPTT regardless of hemostatic protection, so the aPTT is meaningless. Treat breakthrough bleeds with FVIII (no inhibitor) or rFVIIa (with inhibitor). Avoid high-dose aPCC (TMA risk).
:::

:::check peds #ddavp-age sec=vwd refs=vwd-mx-2021
An 18-month-old with type 1 VWD needs a dental procedure. Why is desmopressin generally avoided at this age?
- [ ] It doesn't raise VWF in toddlers
- [x] Risk of severe hyponatremia and seizures
- [ ] It causes thrombocytopenia in all children
- [ ] It's contraindicated in type 1 VWD
> DDAVP's antidiuretic effect causes water retention and hyponatremia, with the greatest seizure risk under age 2. Use TXA and/or VWF concentrate.
:::

:::check both #vwd-2b sec=vwd refs=vwd-dx-2021
A patient with VWD has mild thrombocytopenia, ↓high-molecular-weight multimers and enhanced aggregation with low-dose ristocetin. Platelets fall further after DDAVP. Which type?
- [ ] Type 1
- [ ] Type 2A
- [x] Type 2B
- [ ] Type 2N
> Type 2B VWF has gain-of-function binding to GPIb, which clears platelets and large multimers. DDAVP releases more abnormal VWF and can worsen thrombocytopenia, so use VWF concentrate.
:::

:::check adult #acq-ha sec=liver-acquired refs=wfh-2020
A 74-year-old with no bleeding history develops large ecchymoses and a forearm hematoma. aPTT 78 s, PT normal; the aPTT corrects immediately on mixing but prolongs after 2 h incubation. FVIII 2%. What is the diagnosis?
- [ ] Lupus anticoagulant
- [ ] Late-onset mild hemophilia A
- [x] Acquired hemophilia A (FVIII autoantibody)
- [ ] Vitamin K deficiency
> FVIII autoantibodies are time- and temperature-dependent, prolonging the aPTT after incubation. Treat with bypassing agents, recombinant porcine FVIII or emicizumab, plus immunosuppression. Screen for underlying malignancy and autoimmunity.
:::

:::check peds #la-child sec=mixing refs=isth-la-2020
A healthy 5-year-old due for tonsillectomy has aPTT 49 s that does not correct on mixing. A dRVVT confirms lupus anticoagulant. He had a viral URI 2 weeks ago and has no bleeding history. What is the best next step?
- [ ] Cancel surgery and start anticoagulation
- [ ] Give plasma before surgery
- [x] Proceed (or repeat after recovery) if PT and bleeding history are normal; transient LA is common and not hemorrhagic
- [ ] Bethesda assay for FVIII inhibitor
> Post-infectious LA in children is common and usually transient, causing neither bleeding nor thrombosis. Check the PT to exclude LA-hypoprothrombinemia, which can bleed.
:::

- [ ] Reassure; infants bruise easily
- [ ] Bleeding labs only; abuse is unlikely
- [x] Child abuse evaluation (skeletal survey, consider neuroimaging) plus bleeding work-up (CBC, PT/aPTT, fibrinogen, VWF panel, FVIII/FIX)

:::check peds #wilms-avws sec=vwd refs=vwd-mx-2021
A 3-year-old with a newly diagnosed Wilms tumor has a prolonged aPTT and low VWF activity before nephrectomy. What is the likely explanation?
- [ ] Inherited type 3 VWD
- [x] Acquired von Willebrand syndrome associated with Wilms tumor
- [ ] Hemophilia B
- [ ] Factor XII deficiency
> Wilms tumor (hyaluronic acid, adsorption of VWF) is a recognized cause of acquired VWS in children. Plan perioperative VWF replacement or DDAVP with hematology. Levels normalize after tumor removal.

:::check peds #hemo-delivery sec=hemophilia refs=wfh-2020
A woman who is a known carrier of severe hemophilia A is in labor with a male fetus. Which plan is recommended?
- [ ] Routine vacuum-assisted delivery is fine
- [x] Avoid vacuum and forceps, send cord blood for FVIII, avoid heel sticks and IM injections until the result is known, and consider a head ultrasound
- [ ] Mandatory elective cesarean in all cases
- [ ] Give the newborn FFP prophylactically
> Instrumental delivery raises the risk of neonatal ICH and cephalohematoma. Cord FVIII is reliable at birth. Cesarean isn't mandatory; plan delivery with hematology and obstetrics.
:::

:::check peds #hemo-b-newborn sec=hemophilia refs=wfh-2020
A newborn boy whose maternal uncle has mild hemophilia B has a cord FIX of 18%. How do you interpret this?
- [x] Not diagnostic: FIX is physiologically low in neonates; retest at ~6 months
- [ ] Confirms mild hemophilia B
- [ ] Excludes hemophilia B
- [ ] Indicates vitamin K deficiency
> FIX is vitamin K–dependent and low at birth, so mild hemophilia B can't be reliably diagnosed in the newborn period. Severe deficiency (< 1%) is identifiable. Genetic testing can resolve it sooner if the familial variant is known.
:::

:::check peds #vwd-2n sec=vwd refs=vwd-dx-2021
A 10-year-old girl with bleeding after tonsillectomy has FVIII 0.18 IU/mL, with normal VWF antigen and activity. Her father and brothers are unaffected. What is the most likely diagnosis?
- [ ] Hemophilia A carrier only
- [x] Type 2N von Willebrand disease
- [ ] Type 1 VWD
- [ ] Factor XI deficiency
> In type 2N, VWF can't bind and protect FVIII, which mimics mild hemophilia A but is autosomal recessive. Confirm with a VWF:FVIII binding assay. It matters because FVIII concentrate alone has a short half-life; use VWF-containing concentrate.
:::

:::check peds #fxi-tonsil sec=rare-factors refs=wfh-2020
A 7-year-old of Ashkenazi Jewish ancestry, scheduled for tonsillectomy, has aPTT 52 s that corrects on mixing. FXI is 22%. Which plan is most appropriate?
- [ ] Proceed without preparation; FXI deficiency doesn't bleed
- [x] Perioperative tranexamic acid ± FXI concentrate or plasma, planned with hematology (tonsillar bed is a high-fibrinolysis site)
- [ ] Cancel surgery permanently
- [ ] DDAVP alone
> Bleeding in FXI deficiency correlates poorly with level but is concentrated at fibrinolytically active sites (mouth, nose, urinary tract). Antifibrinolytics are central.
:::

- [ ] ≥ 1
- [x] ≥ 3
- [ ] ≥ 6
