:::check peds #nbs-fsa sec=genetics-nbs refs=nhlbi-scd-2014
A newborn screen reports "FSA". The infant has not been transfused. What is the most likely genotype?
- [ ] Sickle cell trait
- [x] HbS/β⁺-thalassemia
- [ ] HbSS
- [ ] HbSC
> Hemoglobins are reported in order of quantity. S > A means HbS/β⁺-thal, a form of SCD that needs penicillin and comprehensive care. Trait would read "FAS" (A > S).
:::

:::check peds #seq-q sec=sequestration refs=nhlbi-scd-2014
A 20-month-old with HbSS is pale and lethargic. The spleen is 6 cm below the costal margin (usually not palpable). Hb 4.6 g/dL (baseline 8.2), retic 14%, platelets 98 × 10⁹/L. What is the treatment?
- [ ] Exchange transfusion to HbS < 30%
- [x] Fluid resuscitation and cautious transfusion in small aliquots
- [ ] Emergency splenectomy
- [ ] IVIG and observation
> Acute splenic sequestration can cause hypovolemic shock. Resuscitate and transfuse in small aliquots: the spleen releases trapped red cells as it shrinks, risking hyperviscosity overshoot. Plan to prevent recurrence.
:::

:::check peds #aplastic-q sec=aplastic-crisis refs=nhlbi-scd-2014
A 6-year-old with HbSS is fatigued and pale. Hb 4.9 g/dL (baseline 8), retic 0.2%, spleen not palpable. Her brother also has HbSS. What is the most likely cause?
- [ ] Splenic sequestration
- [x] Parvovirus B19 transient aplastic crisis
- [ ] Delayed hemolytic transfusion reaction
- [ ] Iron deficiency
> Reticulocytopenia with acute anemia in chronic hemolysis is parvovirus aplastic crisis. Transfuse, use droplet precautions, and check the sibling.
:::

:::check peds #acs-q sec=acs refs=nhlbi-scd-2014,nacssg
On day 2 of admission for back pain, a 10-year-old with HbSS develops fever 38.9 °C, SpO₂ 91% and a new right lower lobe infiltrate. Hb 7.4 g/dL (baseline 8.6). Which regimen is most appropriate?
- [ ] Ceftriaxone alone and IV fluids at 1.5× maintenance
- [x] Ceftriaxone plus azithromycin, O₂, incentive spirometry, and simple transfusion
- [ ] Immediate exchange transfusion for all ACS
- [ ] Systemic corticosteroids
> Treat ACS with a cephalosporin plus a macrolide (atypicals), oxygen, spirometry, careful fluids and simple transfusion (Hb > 1 g/dL below baseline). Escalate to exchange for progression or severe hypoxemia.
:::

:::check peds #hu-infant sec=hydroxyurea refs=nhlbi-scd-2014,baby-hug
Parents of an asymptomatic 9-month-old with HbSS ask about hydroxyurea. What does the evidence-based guidance say?
- [ ] Wait until two pain crises occur
- [ ] Start only after age 2
- [x] Offer hydroxyurea now, regardless of clinical severity
- [ ] Use only if a TCD is abnormal
> NHLBI 2014 recommends offering hydroxyurea to all infants ≥ 9 months with HbSS or HbS/β⁰ regardless of severity. BABY HUG showed fewer pain episodes, ACS episodes and transfusions with good safety.
:::

:::check peds #priapism-q sec=priapism refs=nhlbi-scd-2014
A 15-year-old with HbSS has had a painful erection for 5 hours despite hydration and analgesia. What is the next step?
- [ ] Exchange transfusion
- [x] Urgent urology: aspiration and irrigation (± phenylephrine)
- [ ] Observe for another 12 hours
- [ ] Ice packs only
> Priapism lasting > 4 h is ischemic and threatens erectile function. Corporal aspiration and irrigation is first-line; transfusion is not.
:::

:::check peds #pcn-dose sec=fever refs=props,nhlbi-scd-2014
What is the appropriate penicillin VK prophylaxis for a 3½-year-old with HbSS and no history of invasive pneumococcal disease?
- [ ] 125 mg once daily
- [ ] 125 mg twice daily
- [x] 250 mg twice daily
- [ ] Stop at age 3
> 125 mg BID from ≤ 2 months to 3 years, then 250 mg BID to age 5 (PROPS). Continuing past 5 is reserved for prior invasive pneumococcal infection or splenectomy.
:::

:::check peds #retinopathy sec=chronic refs=nhlbi-scd-2014
For which genotype is proliferative sickle retinopathy most common, so annual dilated eye exams from age 10 are especially important?
- [ ] HbAS
- [ ] HbSS
- [x] HbSC
- [ ] HbAC
> Retinopathy is more frequent in HbSC (higher Hct and viscosity) than in HbSS. NHLBI recommends dilated retinal exams starting at age 10 for all SCD genotypes.
:::

:::check both #gene-tx-2026 sec=curative refs=casgevy-peds-2026,voxelotor-withdrawal
Which statement about disease-modifying or curative SCD therapy is correct as of late 2026?
- [ ] Voxelotor is first-line for hemolytic anemia in children
- [x] Exa-cel (CRISPR) is FDA-approved down to age 2 and requires myeloablative busulfan conditioning
- [ ] Gene therapies don't affect fertility
- [ ] Crizanlizumab clearly reduced crises in phase 3
> Exa-cel's US label was expanded to ≥ 2 years in July 2026. Busulfan conditioning causes infertility, so fertility preservation is offered. Voxelotor was withdrawn in 2024. Crizanlizumab's phase 3 STAND trial was negative.
:::

:::check both #cis-trans sec=alpha-thal refs=tif-2021
Both partners have α-thal trait. Which combination puts their fetus at risk of Hb Barts hydrops fetalis?
- [ ] Both −α/−α (trans)
- [x] Both −−/αα (cis)
- [ ] One −α/αα and one −α/−α
- [ ] One −α/−α and one −−/αα
> Hydrops needs four deleted genes (−−/−−). Only when both parents carry a cis deletion (−−) can a child inherit two −− chromosomes (25% risk). Offer prenatal diagnosis.
:::

:::check both #trait-hematuria sec=trait refs=nhlbi-scd-2014
A 22-year-old with sickle cell trait has painless gross hematuria and flank pain. Which diagnosis must be excluded urgently?
- [ ] Simple papillary necrosis only
- [ ] IgA nephropathy
- [x] Renal medullary carcinoma
- [ ] Nephrolithiasis
> Renal medullary carcinoma is an aggressive tumor almost exclusive to people with sickle trait or SCD. Hematuria in trait warrants imaging (CT/MRI) rather than assuming papillary necrosis.
:::
