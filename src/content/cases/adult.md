# Platelets falling after surgery {adult #hit-case module=platelets refs=4ts,ash-hit-2018}
A post-operative platelet drop. Score it, act on it, and avoid the classic traps.

## Day 8 after CABG
A 66-year-old on prophylactic UFH since surgery has platelets **82 × 10⁹/L**, down from 240 on day 3. His left arm is swollen. No other new drugs, no sepsis.

:::check
What is the 4Ts score?
- [ ] 3: low
- [ ] 5: intermediate
- [x] 7–8: high
- [ ] Can't be calculated without the ELISA
> Fall > 50% with nadir ≥ 20 = 2; onset day 5–10 = 2; suspected or new thrombosis (arm swelling) = 1–2; no other cause = 2. That's high pretest probability.
:::

## Action
:::check
What is the immediate management?
- [ ] Switch UFH to enoxaparin
- [ ] Transfuse platelets and continue heparin
- [x] Stop all heparin (including flushes), start argatroban or bivalirudin (or fondaparinux/DOAC if stable), send PF4 immunoassay, ultrasound limbs
- [ ] Start warfarin today
> Cross-reactivity makes LMWH unsafe. Warfarin before platelet recovery causes venous limb gangrene. Platelet transfusion is for bleeding only.
:::

## Results
PF4 ELISA OD 2.1; SRA positive. Upper-extremity DVT confirmed.

:::check
How long should he be anticoagulated?
- [ ] Until platelets recover only
- [x] About 3 months (HIT with thrombosis), transitioning to a DOAC or to warfarin once platelets are ≥ 150 × 10⁹/L
- [ ] Lifelong
- [ ] 2 weeks
> HIT with thrombosis is treated like provoked VTE: ~3 months. Document a heparin allergy. Brief intraoperative heparin re-exposure may be possible years later once antibodies have cleared.
:::

# Confusion and purpura {adult #ttp-case module=hemolysis refs=plasmic,isth-ttp-2020,hercules}
A young woman with neurologic symptoms and a low platelet count.

## Presentation
A 31-year-old has headache and word-finding difficulty, with petechiae. Platelets **11 × 10⁹/L**, Hb 8.4 g/dL, **schistocytes 4%**, LDH 1,280 U/L, indirect bilirubin 2.6 mg/dL, creatinine 1.0 mg/dL, INR 1.0, MCV 91 fL. No cancer, transplant or new drugs.

:::check
What is her PLASMIC score and its meaning?
- [ ] 4: low probability; look for other causes
- [ ] 5: intermediate
- [x] 6: high probability of severe ADAMTS13 deficiency
- [ ] 7: maximum score
> One point each for platelets < 30, hemolysis (indirect bilirubin > 2), no active cancer, no transplant, INR < 1.5, and creatinine < 2.0. MCV is 91, so the "MCV < 90" item scores 0. A total of 6 is high probability (≥ 6). Treat as iTTP now.
:::

## Treatment
:::check
What should start immediately?
- [ ] Platelet transfusion to > 50 × 10⁹/L
- [x] Therapeutic plasma exchange plus corticosteroids plus caplacizumab; send ADAMTS13 first
- [ ] Eculizumab
- [ ] IVIG alone
> Draw ADAMTS13 before plasma exchange, then start iTTP therapy. Caplacizumab speeds platelet recovery and reduces exacerbations. Rituximab reduces relapse.
:::

## Day 3
ADAMTS13 activity **< 5%** with an inhibitor. Platelets are 98 × 10⁹/L.

**Teaching points**
- Continue caplacizumab until ADAMTS13 recovers.
- Monitor ADAMTS13 during remission: preemptive rituximab if it falls again.
- Future pregnancies need planning with hematology.

# Back pain and a high protein gap {adult #myeloma-case module=lymphoma refs=imwg-2014,perseus}
Myeloma: diagnosis by SLiM-CRAB, and what not to do.

## Presentation
A 64-year-old has had back pain for 3 months. Ca **11.6 mg/dL**, creatinine **2.3 mg/dL**, Hb **9.4 g/dL**, total protein **9.6 g/dL**, albumin **3.0 g/dL**. The smear shows **rouleaux**.

:::check
What is the most useful next test?
- [ ] Bone scan
- [x] SPEP with immunofixation and serum free light chains
- [ ] PSA
- [ ] Bone marrow first, before any blood tests
> Hypercalcemia, renal failure, anemia, back pain and a protein gap → myeloma. Use low-dose whole-body CT or PET-CT for bones; bone scans miss lytic lesions.
:::

## Results
IgG-κ M-spike 4.1 g/dL, κ/λ ratio 160. Marrow: 45% clonal plasma cells. CT: multiple lytic lesions.

:::check
Which statement is correct?
- [ ] Smoldering myeloma; observe
- [x] Multiple myeloma (CRAB plus FLC ratio ≥ 100); start therapy, give a bone-modifying agent, protect the kidneys, and avoid NSAIDs and IV contrast where possible
- [ ] MGUS
- [ ] Waldenström
> Any myeloma-defining event triggers therapy. For transplant-eligible patients, quadruplet daratumumab-VRd induction (PERSEUS) then ASCT is standard. Hydrate and treat hypercalcemia now.
:::

# Gum bleeding and abnormal promyelocytes {both #apl-case module=leukemia refs=apl0406,aaml1331}
The most curable leukemia, and the one that kills fastest if missed.

## Presentation
A 23-year-old (or a 15-year-old: the principles are the same) has gum bleeding and bruising. WBC **2.8 × 10⁹/L**, platelets **19 × 10⁹/L**, **fibrinogen 68 mg/dL**, INR 1.9. The smear shows hypergranular promyelocytes with bundles of Auer rods.

:::check
When should ATRA be started?
- [ ] After PML::RARA is confirmed
- [x] Immediately, on morphologic suspicion
- [ ] After marrow biopsy
- [ ] Only if WBC > 10 × 10⁹/L
> Early hemorrhagic death is the main threat in APL. Starting ATRA on suspicion and aggressively replacing fibrinogen (cryo) and platelets saves lives.
:::

## Treatment choice
PML::RARA confirmed. WBC remains < 10 × 10⁹/L.

:::check
What is the preferred regimen?
- [ ] 7+3 chemotherapy
- [x] ATRA plus arsenic trioxide (chemotherapy-free), with QTc and electrolyte monitoring
- [ ] Allogeneic HSCT in first remission
- [ ] Hydroxyurea
> For non-high-risk APL, ATRA + ATO beat ATRA + chemotherapy (APL0406). Pediatric AAML1331 achieved > 95% EFS with minimal or no anthracycline. Keep K > 4 and Mg > 2 on ATO.
:::

## Day 8
Fever, dyspnea, effusions and weight gain.

:::check
What is the most likely problem, and the treatment?
- [ ] Pneumonia: antibiotics only
- [x] Differentiation syndrome: dexamethasone immediately (hold ATRA/ATO if severe)
- [ ] TACO: diuretics only
- [ ] Leukostasis: leukapheresis
> Treat differentiation syndrome at the first signs. Infection can coexist, so culture and cover if indicated.
:::

# Short of breath during a transfusion {adult #dyspnea-transfusion module=transfusion refs=trali-2019,taco-2018}
TRALI or TACO? The bedside findings decide.

## Scenario A
A 79-year-old with CKD and heart failure is on her second RBC unit. After 2 hours she is **dyspneic, BP 176/94**, JVP raised, crackles, NT-proBNP doubled.

:::check
What is the diagnosis and treatment?
- [ ] TRALI: supportive care, avoid diuretics
- [x] TACO: stop, sit upright, oxygen, IV furosemide
- [ ] Anaphylaxis: epinephrine
- [ ] Acute hemolytic reaction
> Hypertension, venous congestion and rising BNP within 12 h is TACO. Prevent it with single-unit orders, slower rates and pre-emptive diuretics in at-risk patients.
:::

## Scenario B
A 45-year-old receiving plasma for liver biopsy develops hypoxemia (SpO₂ 82%) at 1 hour, with **bilateral infiltrates, BP 86/50, T 38.3 °C**, normal JVP and BNP.

:::check
Which response is correct?
- [ ] Furosemide 40 mg IV
- [x] Stop, respiratory support (may need ventilation), avoid diuretics, and report to the blood supplier for donor antibody investigation
- [ ] Restart the plasma more slowly
- [ ] Antihistamine and continue
> TRALI: acute lung injury within 6 h without left atrial hypertension, often with hypotension and fever. Donor anti-HLA/HNA antibodies are implicated, and reporting protects future recipients.
:::
