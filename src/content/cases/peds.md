# The pale, milk-loving toddler {peds #pale-toddler module=anemia refs=aap-iron-2026,powers-2017}
A 20-month-old with pallor and irritability. Work out the cause, the treatment, and what follow-up proves you right.

## Presentation
Mateo, 20 months, is brought in because his grandmother noticed he looks "washed out". He drinks **~40 oz of whole milk a day** from a bottle, eats little solid food, and is irritable. Growth is on the 25th percentile. HR 128, otherwise stable. Pale conjunctivae, soft flow murmur, **no hepatosplenomegaly or lymphadenopathy**.

:::check
Which initial tests are most useful?
- [ ] Hemoglobin electrophoresis and bone marrow
- [x] CBC with indices, reticulocyte count, ferritin (± CRP), and a blood lead level
- [ ] Coombs test and haptoglobin
- [ ] B12 and folate
> Milk excess and a low-iron diet make iron deficiency most likely. AAP 2026 pairs the CBC with ferritin. Lead shares risk factors and worsens with iron deficiency, so screen at the same time.
:::

## Results
Hb **6.9 g/dL**, MCV **59 fL**, RDW **19%**, RBC 3.6 × 10¹²/L, platelets **520 × 10⁹/L**, retic 1.0%. Ferritin **3 ng/mL**, CRP normal, lead 2.0 µg/dL.

:::check
What best explains this picture?
- [ ] β-thalassemia trait (Mentzer index < 13)
- [x] Iron deficiency anemia with reactive thrombocytosis (Mentzer ≈ 16)
- [ ] Anemia of inflammation
- [ ] Lead poisoning
> Mentzer = 59 / 3.6 ≈ 16 (> 13 suggests IDA). High RDW and very low ferritin confirm it. Reactive thrombocytosis is common in IDA and needs no treatment.
:::

## Management
He is alert, feeding and hemodynamically stable.

:::check
What is the best plan?
- [ ] Admit for 15 mL/kg RBC transfusion over 2 h
- [x] Oral elemental iron 3 mg/kg once daily away from milk, limit milk to ≤ 16–24 oz/day from a cup, recheck at ~4 weeks
- [ ] IV ferric carboxymaltose today
- [ ] Iron 6 mg/kg divided three times daily with milk to improve tolerance
> Once-daily low-dose ferrous sulfate is effective (Powers 2017). Fixing the milk intake is half the treatment. Compensated chronic anemia doesn't need transfusion. If it ever does, give small aliquots slowly.
:::

## Follow-up
At 4 weeks: Hb **9.4 g/dL** (+2.5), retic surged at day 7, milk down to 16 oz/day.

**Teaching points**
- An Hb rise ≥ 1–2 g/dL at 4 weeks **confirms the diagnosis**. No rise → check adherence and administration, then ongoing loss, celiac/*H. pylori*, thal trait, IRIDA.
- **Treat ~3 months total** and recheck ferritin.
- Infant iron deficiency harms neurodevelopment even without anemia, so prevention (supplementing breastfed infants, no cow's milk before 12 months) matters.

# Petechiae after a cold {peds #petechiae module=platelets refs=ash-itp-2019}
A 3-year-old with sudden bruising. Decide ITP vs leukemia, and when to treat.

## Presentation
Aisha, 3, developed petechiae on her legs and trunk and several bruises over 24 h. She had a URI 2 weeks ago. She is playful.

:::check
Which finding would most strongly argue **against** primary ITP and require a marrow exam?
- [ ] Platelet count of 4 × 10⁹/L
- [ ] Wet purpura in the mouth
- [x] Hepatosplenomegaly or an abnormal Hb/WBC
- [ ] Recent viral illness
> ITP is isolated thrombocytopenia in an otherwise normal child. Organomegaly, lymphadenopathy, bone pain, other cytopenias or an abnormal smear point to leukemia or marrow failure.
:::

## Results
Platelets **6 × 10⁹/L**, Hb 12.1 g/dL, WBC 7.8 × 10⁹/L with normal differential. Smear: **large platelets**, normal RBCs and WBCs. Exam: no organomegaly. Bleeding is skin-only.

:::check
What do ASH guidelines recommend?
- [x] Observation with outpatient follow-up, regardless of the count
- [ ] IVIG 1 g/kg today because the count is < 10 × 10⁹/L
- [ ] Prednisone for 4 weeks
- [ ] Bone marrow before deciding
> For no or minor (skin) bleeding, observation is recommended. Counsel the family: avoid NSAIDs and contact sports, and return for mucosal bleeding, head injury, headache or vomiting.
:::

## Day 4
She returns with **epistaxis lasting 30 minutes** that needed packing. Vital signs are stable; platelets 5 × 10⁹/L.

:::check
What now?
- [ ] Platelet transfusion
- [x] Short course of prednisone (2–4 mg/kg/day, max 120 mg, 5–7 days), or IVIG if a faster rise is needed
- [ ] Splenectomy referral
- [ ] Rituximab
> Clinically significant mucosal bleeding warrants treatment. ASH suggests steroids over IVIG/anti-D, without prolonged courses. Platelets are for life-threatening bleeding.
:::

## Outcome
Platelets 85 × 10⁹/L after a week, normal at 6 weeks.

**Teaching points**
- ~70–80% of children remit within 12 months. Chronic ITP is commoner in adolescents.
- ICH risk is ~0.5%, concentrated at counts < 10–20 × 10⁹/L with trauma.
- Treat the bleeding, not the number.

# Limp and night pain {peds #limp module=leukemia refs=aall1731}
A preschooler with bone pain whose labs are "a little off". Recognize the leukemia mimic.

## Presentation
Leo, 4, has limped for 3 weeks, wakes at night with leg pain, and has had intermittent low-grade fevers. An X-ray of the hip was normal. An orthopedist suggested transient synovitis or JIA.

:::check
What is the most important next step?
- [ ] MRI of the hip under sedation
- [x] CBC with manual smear review, LDH and uric acid
- [ ] Start naproxen and review in 2 weeks
- [ ] Rheumatology referral and ANA
> Night pain, fever and a prolonged limp are red flags for leukemia (or infection). The CBC/smear plus LDH and uric acid are the fastest screens.
:::

## Results
Hb **8.2 g/dL**, WBC 3.1 × 10⁹/L, ANC **0.5**, platelets **72 × 10⁹/L**; no blasts reported by the analyzer. LDH **1,100 U/L**, uric acid 8.1 mg/dL. Rheumatology suggests prednisone for presumed systemic JIA.

:::check
What do you do?
- [ ] Start prednisone 2 mg/kg and recheck the CBC in 1 week
- [x] No steroids; same-day hematology/oncology, peripheral flow cytometry and bone marrow
- [ ] Repeat the CBC in 1 month
- [ ] Give G-CSF for neutropenia
> Cytopenias in two lines with bone pain and high LDH mean ALL until proven otherwise. Steroids before diagnosis can mask leukemia and compromise risk stratification.
:::

## Diagnosis
Marrow: **B-ALL**, 88% blasts, **high hyperdiploidy**. CSF negative. NCI standard risk (age 4, WBC < 50).

:::check
Which single factor best predicts his relapse risk from here?
- [ ] Initial WBC
- [ ] Sex
- [x] End-of-induction minimal residual disease (MRD)
- [ ] Initial platelet count
> End-of-induction MRD is the strongest prognostic factor and adjusts therapy intensity. In AALL1731, blinatumomab added to chemotherapy improved 3-year DFS to ~96% in standard-risk B-ALL.
:::

## Takeaways
- Get **TPMT/NUDT15 genotyping** before thiopurines, an **echo** before anthracyclines, and a **CXR before any sedation**.
- During induction, **fever with neutropenia** means antibiotics within 60 minutes.

# Fever in a toddler with sickle cell disease {peds #scd-fever module=hemoglobinopathies refs=nhlbi-scd-2014,props}
A 13-month-old with HbSS and a fever. Two emergencies can hide in one visit.

## Presentation
Kofi, 13 months, HbSS, has **T 39.4 °C** for 4 hours. He's fussy but consolable. His mother says she ran out of penicillin a month ago.

:::check
What is the first priority?
- [ ] Wait for the CBC to decide on antibiotics
- [x] Blood culture and parenteral ceftriaxone within 60 minutes
- [ ] Oral amoxicillin and discharge
- [ ] Chest X-ray first
> Functional asplenia makes fever an emergency regardless of how well the child looks. Draw a culture, CBC and retic and give ceftriaxone without waiting for results.
:::

## Results
WBC **31 × 10⁹/L**, Hb **6.1 g/dL** (baseline 8.0), retic **12%**, platelets 110 × 10⁹/L. The spleen is now **4 cm below the costal margin**; last month it was not palpable.

:::check
What other process is developing?
- [ ] Aplastic crisis
- [x] Acute splenic sequestration
- [ ] Iron deficiency
- [ ] Delayed hemolytic transfusion reaction
> An Hb drop ≥ 2 g/dL from baseline with an acutely enlarging spleen and high retic is sequestration. Admit, monitor closely, and give cautious small-aliquot transfusion. Shock can develop within hours.
:::

## Day 2
The blood culture grows ***Streptococcus pneumoniae***. Kofi is improving on IV antibiotics; Hb is stable after transfusion.

:::check
Which prevention step has the strongest evidence for his age?
- [ ] Monthly IVIG
- [x] Penicillin VK 125 mg twice daily until age 3 (then 250 mg BID to age 5), plus completed pneumococcal and meningococcal vaccines
- [ ] Splenectomy now
- [ ] Daily folic acid
> PROPS showed an 84% reduction in pneumococcal infection. Adherence lapses are a common cause of invasive disease. Re-educate the family and arrange easy refills.

**Hydroxyurea:** offer it now (≥ 9 months, regardless of severity). Teach the parents **spleen palpation** and the **fever plan** (go to the ED for T ≥ 38.5 °C).

- [ ] Aspirin
- [ ] MRI, and treat only if an infarct is present

- [ ] Bone marrow transplant is the only option

# Bloody diarrhea after a farm visit {peds #stec module=hemolysis refs=wong-stec-2000,ardissino-2016}
Prevent and recognize STEC-HUS.

## Day 3 of illness
Noah, 3, has had **bloody diarrhea** for 2 days after a petting-zoo visit. He's afebrile and mildly dehydrated. Stool is **positive for Shiga toxin**. Creatinine and CBC are normal.

:::check
What is the best management?
- [ ] Azithromycin to clear the organism
- [ ] Loperamide for comfort
- [x] Isotonic IV fluids (volume expansion) and daily CBC/creatinine
- [ ] Discharge; follow up only if symptoms worsen
> Antibiotics and antimotility drugs raise HUS risk in children. Early volume expansion is associated with less oliguria and dialysis. HUS typically appears 5–10 days after diarrhea begins.
:::

## Day 7
Hb **7.2 g/dL** with **schistocytes**, platelets **42 × 10⁹/L**, LDH 2,100 U/L, creatinine **2.3 mg/dL** with falling urine output.

:::check
Which statement is correct?
- [ ] Start plasma exchange as for TTP
- [x] This is STEC-HUS: supportive care, RBCs for symptomatic anemia, dialysis as needed; avoid prophylactic platelet transfusion
- [ ] Give eculizumab to all STEC-HUS
- [ ] Start high-dose steroids
> STEC-HUS is managed supportively. About half need dialysis; most recover. Platelets are reserved for bleeding or procedures.
:::

## When to rethink the diagnosis
**Teaching points**
- No diarrhea or STEC, a relapsing or familial course, or very low C3 → **complement-mediated HUS** (eculizumab/ravulizumab).
- A child < 2 with invasive pneumococcal disease → **pneumococcal HUS** (avoid plasma).
- An infant → **cobalamin C** (check homocysteine and MMA).
