## Approach to thrombocytopenia {both #approach refs=nathan-oski,aabb-plt-2025}

:::algo
1. **Is it real?** Rule out **EDTA pseudothrombocytopenia** (clumps on smear) by redrawing in citrate or heparin, and look at the feathered edge.
2. **Isolated, or with other cytopenias?** Other lines affected → **marrow process** (leukemia, aplasia, B12) or consumption (DIC, TMA) or hypersplenism.
3. **Well or sick?** Well → ITP, drugs, inherited. Sick → sepsis, DIC, TMA, HIT, marrow infiltration.
4. **Platelet size** (smear/MPV/IPF):
   - **Large:** consumption (ITP), Bernard-Soulier, MYH9.
   - **Small:** **Wiskott-Aldrich**/XLT.
5. **Smear:** schistocytes (TMA/DIC), blasts, atypical lymphocytes, giant platelets, Döhle-like inclusions.
:::

**Bleeding risk depends on the mechanism, not just the count.**
- In ITP, young, highly functional platelets mean serious bleeding is uncommon above ~20 × 10⁹/L.
- Chemotherapy-induced (hypoproliferative) thrombocytopenia carries more risk at the same count. Prophylactic transfusion threshold is 10 × 10⁹/L [@aabb-plt-2025].
- **Mucocutaneous bleeding** (petechiae, epistaxis, gum and menstrual bleeding) is the platelet/VWF pattern.

## Pediatric ITP {peds #itp-peds refs=ash-itp-2019}

- **Definition:** isolated platelets < 100 × 10⁹/L with no other cause. **Newly diagnosed < 3 months; persistent 3–12 months; chronic > 12 months.**
- **Typical child:** age 2–6, abrupt petechiae/bruising days to weeks after a viral illness (rarely after MMR), **otherwise well, normal exam** (no hepatosplenomegaly, lymphadenopathy or bone pain), **normal Hb and WBC**, smear with **large platelets only**.
- **Bone marrow exam is not needed when features are typical**, even before steroids. **Any atypical feature** (other cytopenias, organomegaly, bone pain, systemic symptoms, abnormal smear) → marrow first.

**ASH 2019 management** [@ash-itp-2019]
- **No or minor (skin-only) bleeding → observation**, regardless of platelet count, with outpatient follow-up.
- **Non-life-threatening mucosal bleeding or impaired quality of life → short-course corticosteroids** (prednisone 2–4 mg/kg/day, max 120 mg/day, for **5–7 days**), preferred over IVIG or anti-D. **No prolonged courses.**
- **When a rapid rise is needed** (active bleeding, procedure): **IVIG 0.8–1 g/kg** (headache, aseptic meningitis), or **anti-D** if Rh(D)-positive, DAT-negative and spleen intact (risk of intravascular hemolysis).
- **Persistent or chronic disease needing treatment:** **TPO receptor agonists** (eltrombopag, romiplostim; approved ≥ 1 year) over rituximab or splenectomy.
- **Life-threatening bleeding** (ICH, major GI): **platelet transfusion** (repeated or continuous) **+ IVIG 1 g/kg + IV methylprednisolone 30 mg/kg** (max 1 g) at once.
- **Course:** ~**70–80% resolve within 12 months**. Chronic disease is likelier in **adolescents**, with insidious onset, and in girls. ICH risk ~0.5%, mostly with counts < 10–20, head trauma or hematuria.
- **Secondary causes to consider in adolescents and chronic ITP:** SLE, HIV/HCV (risk-based), CVID, **ALPS/Evans syndrome**, Wiskott-Aldrich in boys with small platelets.

## Adult ITP: the 2026 ASH update {adult #itp-adult refs=ash-itp-2026,ash-itp-2019,rilzabrutinib}

- **Work-up:** CBC/smear; **HIV, HCV**, consider *H. pylori*; quantitative immunoglobulins; pregnancy test; review medications. Marrow only if atypical or age > 60 with other abnormalities.
- **Treat newly diagnosed ITP with platelets < 30 × 10⁹/L** (2019 recommendation, unchanged). **Prednisone ≤ 6 weeks** (0.5–2 mg/kg/day) or **dexamethasone 40 mg/day × 4 days** (both unchanged).

:::update ASH 2026: combination initial therapy, TPO agents second-line {year=2026}
**Initial therapy:** ASH now **suggests corticosteroids plus rituximab, or corticosteroids plus a thrombopoietic agent** (± IVIG), over corticosteroids alone. These combinations gave better sustained responses and less rescue therapy. If rituximab and TPO agents are unavailable, use corticosteroids alone rather than steroids plus MMF.

**After corticosteroids:** ASH **recommends a thrombopoietic agent** (eltrombopag, romiplostim, avatrombopag) and **suggests rituximab** for those wishing to avoid long-term therapy. For patients ineligible for or declining these, it suggests a **BTK inhibitor (rilzabrutinib), MMF, or a SYK inhibitor (fostamatinib)** [@ash-itp-2026].

This replaces 2019 recommendations 5–9, including splenectomy as a routine second-line choice. Rilzabrutinib was FDA-approved in Aug 2025 for persistent/chronic ITP [@rilzabrutinib].
:::

- **Splenectomy** remains an option for refractory disease, ideally **≥ 12 months after diagnosis** (spontaneous remissions occur). Vaccinate beforehand.
- **Pregnancy:**
  - **Gestational thrombocytopenia:** mild (usually > 70), late, harmless.
  - **Preeclampsia/HELLP** (hypertension, LFTs, hemolysis).
  - **ITP** (any trimester, can be severe): steroids or IVIG. The neonate needs monitoring (see [Neonatal](/learn/neonatal#neonatal-thrombocytopenia)).

## Disseminated intravascular coagulation {both #dic refs=isth-dic}

- **Always secondary:** sepsis (most common), trauma/TBI, malignancy (**APL**, adenocarcinoma), obstetric catastrophes, envenomation. [[peds]] Add **meningococcemia/purpura fulminans** and **Kasabach-Merritt**.
- **Labs:** ↓platelets (falling trend matters), ↑PT/aPTT, **↓fibrinogen** (late; often normal early in sepsis because it is an acute-phase reactant), **↑↑D-dimer**, schistocytes.
- **ISTH overt DIC score ≥ 5:**

| Item | Points |
|---|---|
| Platelets > 100 / 50–100 / < 50 × 10⁹/L | 0 / 1 / 2 |
| D-dimer or FDP: none / moderate / strong increase | 0 / 2 / 3 |
| PT prolonged < 3 s / 3–6 s / > 6 s | 0 / 1 / 2 |
| Fibrinogen > 1.0 / ≤ 1.0 g/L | 0 / 1 |

- **Management:** **treat the cause.** Replace products **for bleeding or procedures**, not numbers alone:
  - Platelets < 50 (bleeding) or < 10–20 (not bleeding).
  - **Cryoprecipitate for fibrinogen < 150 mg/dL with bleeding.**
  - Plasma 10–15 mL/kg.
  - Heparin for thrombosis-predominant DIC (purpura fulminans, large-vessel thrombosis).

Use the [DIC score calculator](/tools/scores).

## Heparin-induced thrombocytopenia {both #hit refs=4ts,ash-hit-2018}

[[adult]] **Common in adults; rare in children** except after cardiac surgery, on ECMO, or in the PICU.

- **Mechanism:** IgG against **PF4–heparin** complexes activates platelets via FcγRIIa, which is **prothrombotic**. Risk: UFH > LMWH; surgical > medical; women > men.
- **Typical course:** platelet fall **> 50%** starting **day 5–10** of heparin. Nadir is usually **20–100 × 10⁹/L**; **bleeding is rare**.
  - **Rapid onset (< 24 h)** if heparin was given in the past 30 (up to 100) days.
  - **Delayed onset** after heparin is stopped.
- **4Ts score** [@4ts]:
  - **0–3 (low): HIT very unlikely (NPV > 99%).** Don't test; continue heparin.
  - 4–5 intermediate, 6–8 high → **stop all heparin** (including flushes and heparin-coated catheters), **start a non-heparin anticoagulant**, send **PF4 immunoassay**, then a **functional assay (SRA/HIPA)** if positive.

**ASH 2018 management** [@ash-hit-2018]
- **Non-heparin anticoagulant even without thrombosis:** argatroban, bivalirudin, fondaparinux, or a **DOAC** in stable patients.
- **No warfarin until platelets ≥ 150 × 10⁹/L**, then overlap ≥ 5 days. If warfarin was already started, give **vitamin K**: warfarin-induced protein C depletion causes **venous limb gangrene**.
- **No prophylactic platelet transfusion** (bleeding or high-risk procedures only).
- **Duration:** isolated HIT — until platelet recovery at minimum (often 4–6 weeks); **HIT with thrombosis — 3 months**.
- **Image for DVT** (lower-extremity ultrasound) in all HIT patients; subclinical thrombosis is common.

## Drug-induced immune thrombocytopenia {both #drug-itp}

- **Onset ~5–10 days** after a new drug, or **within hours** on re-exposure or with GP IIb/IIIa inhibitors. The count is often **< 20 × 10⁹/L** with mucosal bleeding.
- **Usual culprits:** **quinine (including tonic water)**, vancomycin, piperacillin, cephalosporins, TMP-SMX, **linezolid**, carbamazepine, **valproate** (dose-related, non-immune), heparin (HIT), GP IIb/IIIa inhibitors, checkpoint inhibitors.
- **Stop the drug.** Platelets recover in ~5–10 days. IVIG or platelets for serious bleeding. Drug-dependent antibody testing exists at reference labs.

## Inherited platelet disorders {peds #inherited refs=nathan-oski}

**Suspect them** with lifelong mucocutaneous bleeding, a family history, abnormal platelet size, syndromic features, or "ITP" refractory to treatment.

| Disorder | Count / size | Defect | Key lab |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Glanzmann thrombasthenia** (AR) | **Normal** | GPIIb/IIIa (αIIbβ3) | **No aggregation to any agonist except ristocetin**; flow CD41/CD61 absent |
| **Bernard-Soulier** (AR) | **Low, giant** | GPIb-IX-V | **Absent ristocetin aggregation, *not* corrected by normal plasma** (corrected in VWD) |
| **Wiskott-Aldrich / XLT** (X-linked) | **Low, small** | WAS protein | **Eczema + infections + microthrombocytopenia**; HSCT or gene therapy |
| **MYH9-related disease** (AD) | Low, giant | Myosin heavy chain IIA | **Döhle-like inclusions**; hearing loss, nephritis, cataracts |
| **RUNX1 / ETV6 / ANKRD26** (AD) | Mildly low | Transcription factors | **Myeloid malignancy risk**: genetic counseling, donor screening |
| Gray platelet syndrome | Low, large, gray | α-granule (NBEAL2) | Myelofibrosis |
| Storage pool / Hermansky-Pudlak | Normal | δ-granules | **Albinism**, pulmonary fibrosis (HPS) |
| CAMT / TAR | Severe neonatal ↓ | MPL / RBM8A | TAR: **absent radii, thumbs present** |

**Management:**
- **Antifibrinolytics** (tranexamic acid) for mucosal bleeding.
- **DDAVP** for some storage pool defects.
- **Platelet transfusion** for major bleeding (HLA-matched if alloimmunized).
- **rFVIIa** for Glanzmann with antibodies.
- **Hormonal control of heavy menstrual bleeding.**

## Thrombocytosis {both #thrombocytosis refs=who-haem5-myeloid}

- [[peds]] **In children > 95% is reactive:** infection, inflammation, **iron deficiency**, asplenia, hemolysis, recovery after marrow suppression, **Kawasaki disease (peaks in weeks 2–3)**.
  - **Even > 1,000 × 10⁹/L is usually reactive in children.**
  - **Reactive thrombocytosis needs no aspirin and doesn't cause thrombosis.**
  - Primary (ET, familial MPL/THPO) is rare.
- [[adult]] Check CRP, ferritin and smear. If persistent and unexplained, test **JAK2, CALR, MPL** and BCR::ABL1 (CML can present as thrombocytosis).

## Kasabach-Merritt phenomenon {peds #kmp}

- **Kaposiform hemangioendothelioma or tufted angioma**, **not** common infantile hemangioma. Platelets are trapped in the lesion, causing **profound thrombocytopenia plus consumptive coagulopathy** (low fibrinogen, high D-dimer). The lesion is rapidly enlarging, firm and purpuric.
- **First-line: sirolimus** (± corticosteroids); vincristine as an alternative.
- **Avoid platelet transfusion unless actively bleeding or for surgery.** It can enlarge the lesion.
