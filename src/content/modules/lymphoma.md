## Lymphadenopathy in children: when to biopsy {peds #lad-peds refs=nathan-oski}

**Palpable nodes are normal in most children.** Normal sizes:
- Cervical/axillary ≤ 1 cm
- Inguinal ≤ 1.5 cm
- Epitrochlear ≤ 0.5 cm
- Palpable supraclavicular nodes are **never** normal.

**Red flags for biopsy**
- **Supraclavicular node** (any size). Right drains the mediastinum; left drains the abdomen.
- Node **> 2 cm** and growing, or not smaller after **4–6 weeks** (or no infectious explanation).
- **Hard, matted, fixed, non-tender** nodes.
- **Systemic symptoms:** fever, drenching night sweats, weight loss > 10%.
- **Abnormal CBC** (cytopenias, blasts), **hepatosplenomegaly**, or **abnormal chest X-ray** (mediastinal mass).

:::algo
1. History: infection, cat scratch, TB exposure, travel, medications.
2. Exam: all node regions, liver, spleen, skin.
3. Infection-focused tests if indicated (EBV, CMV, *Bartonella*, TB). **Consider a trial of antibiotics only for an acutely tender, inflamed node.**
4. Red flags → **CBC/smear, LDH, uric acid, CXR**, then **excisional biopsy** of the most abnormal node. Core biopsy is acceptable if excision isn't feasible; **FNA alone is insufficient.**
5. **Avoid empiric steroids before tissue diagnosis.**
:::

:::check
A 14-year-old has a 2.5-cm firm, non-tender left supraclavicular node for 3 weeks, plus night sweats. What is the next step?
- [ ] Two-week trial of amoxicillin-clavulanate
- [ ] Reassess in 6 weeks
- [x] CBC, LDH, chest X-ray and excisional biopsy
- [ ] Fine-needle aspiration only
> A supraclavicular node with B symptoms has a high risk of malignancy (Hodgkin, NHL, metastatic disease). Get a CXR before any sedation for biopsy.
:::

## Hodgkin lymphoma {both #hodgkin refs=ahod1331,s1826,who-haem5-lymphoid}

- **Classic HL:** **Hodgkin/Reed-Sternberg cells (CD30+, CD15+, CD45−, PAX5 dim)** in an inflammatory background. Subtypes: nodular sclerosis (most common in adolescents/young adults, mediastinal), mixed cellularity (EBV, younger children and older adults), lymphocyte-rich, lymphocyte-depleted.
- **Nodular lymphocyte-predominant** (CD20+ "popcorn" LP cells) is biologically distinct and more indolent. The ICC calls it *nodular lymphocyte predominant B-cell lymphoma*.
- **Age:** bimodal peaks at **15–35** and > 55. **The most common cancer in adolescents aged 15–19.**
- **Presentation:** painless cervical/supraclavicular nodes, **mediastinal mass**, **B symptoms**, pruritus, alcohol-induced node pain (rare). Spread is **contiguous**.
- **Staging:** PET-CT (Lugano). Interim PET drives response-adapted therapy.

:::update Pediatric/AYA Hodgkin treatment has moved {year=2024}
- **AHOD1331 (high-risk pediatric HL):** **brentuximab vedotin + AVEPC** improved EFS over ABVE-PC, with less radiation [@ahod1331].
- **S1826 (stage III–IV, age ≥ 12):** **nivolumab + AVD** improved PFS over BV-AVD, with less toxicity [@s1826]. N-AVD is now a standard option for adolescents and adults with advanced disease.
:::

**Survivorship** [[peds]]: cure rates exceed 90%, so late effects matter.
- **Breast cancer after chest radiation in girls**: earlier MRI screening.
- Cardiac disease (anthracyclines, radiation), thyroid disease, infertility, second malignancies.
- Follow COG long-term follow-up guidelines.

## Pediatric non-Hodgkin lymphoma {peds #nhl-peds refs=inter-b-nhl,who-haem5-lymphoid}

**Almost always high-grade and fast-growing.** Indolent lymphomas are rare in children.

| Type | Hallmarks | Treatment notes |
|---|---|---|
| **Burkitt** | **MYC** translocation; "starry sky"; sporadic form: **abdominal mass / ileocecal intussusception**; endemic form: jaw (EBV); **doubling time ~24 h** | **Very high TLS risk**; intensive short chemotherapy **+ rituximab** for high-risk mature B-NHL (Inter-B-NHL ritux 2010) [@inter-b-nhl]; cure > 90% |
| DLBCL / **PMBCL** | Adolescents; PMBCL: anterior mediastinal mass, SVC syndrome | Rituximab-based chemotherapy (e.g., DA-EPOCH-R for PMBCL) |
| **Lymphoblastic lymphoma** (mostly T) | **Mediastinal mass, pleural effusion**, < 25% marrow blasts (≥ 25% is ALL) | ALL-type therapy |
| **ALCL** (ALK+) | CD30+, **NPM1::ALK**; nodes, skin, bone; B symptoms | Chemotherapy; **ALK inhibitors** (crizotinib) and brentuximab for relapse |

## Adult non-Hodgkin lymphoma {adult #nhl-adult refs=polarix,who-haem5-lymphoid}

- **DLBCL** (most common NHL):
  - R-CHOP, or **Pola-R-CHP for IPI ≥ 2** (POLARIX: better PFS) [@polarix].
  - **CAR-T in second line for early relapse/refractory disease.**
  - Bispecific antibodies (glofitamab, epcoritamab) later.
  - CNS prophylaxis decisions use CNS-IPI.
- **Follicular lymphoma:** **t(14;18) BCL2**; indolent. **Watch and wait if low tumor burden** (GELF criteria). Rituximab ± chemotherapy when treatment is needed. Watch for transformation (rising LDH, a fast-growing node).
- **Marginal zone / MALT:** **eradicate *H. pylori* for gastric MALT** (often curative in early stage).
- **Mantle cell:** **t(11;14) cyclin D1**, SOX11+; BTK inhibitors are central; TP53 mutation is high-risk.
- **Burkitt (adult):** DA-EPOCH-R or CODOX-M/IVAC, with TLS prophylaxis.
- **PTLD** [[both]]: EBV-driven after solid organ transplant or HSCT. Reduce immunosuppression; rituximab; EBV-specific T cells (tabelecleucel, EU).

## MGUS and smoldering myeloma {adult #mgus refs=imwg-2014}

| | M-protein | Clonal marrow PCs | Myeloma-defining event |
|---|---|---|---|
| **MGUS** (non-IgM) | **< 3 g/dL** | **< 10%** | **None** |
| **Smoldering myeloma** | ≥ 3 g/dL (or urinary ≥ 500 mg/24 h) | **and/or 10–59%** | None |
| **Multiple myeloma** | Any | **≥ 10%** (or plasmacytoma) | **≥ 1 (CRAB or SLiM)** |

- MGUS progresses at **~1% per year**. Risk-stratify with M-spike size, non-IgG isotype and abnormal FLC ratio. Follow with SPEP/FLC.
- **High-risk smoldering myeloma:** early therapy is being adopted (e.g., daratumumab monotherapy delayed progression). Close monitoring otherwise.

## Multiple myeloma {adult #myeloma refs=imwg-2014,perseus}

**IMWG myeloma-defining events** [@imwg-2014]

:::mnemonic SLiM-CRAB
- **S**ixty: ≥ 60% clonal plasma cells
- **Li**ght chains: involved/uninvolved FLC ratio ≥ 100
- **M**RI: > 1 focal lesion ≥ 5 mm
- **C**alcium > 11 mg/dL (or > 1 mg/dL above ULN)
- **R**enal: creatinine > 2 mg/dL or CrCl < 40 mL/min
- **A**nemia: Hb < 10 g/dL (or > 2 below LLN)
- **B**one: ≥ 1 lytic lesion on CT/PET-CT/skeletal survey
:::

- **Work-up:**
  - **SPEP + immunofixation, serum free light chains** (UPEP optional).
  - **Whole-body low-dose CT or PET-CT** (skeletal survey is less sensitive).
  - Marrow with FISH: high-risk del(17p), t(4;14), t(14;16), 1q gain.
  - R-ISS / R2-ISS staging.
- **Clues:**
  - **Rouleaux**, elevated total protein with a big **protein–albumin gap**.
  - Hypercalcemia with **low anion gap**, renal failure (cast nephropathy).
  - Recurrent infections, back pain.
  - Hyperviscosity (IgA, IgG3).
- **Transplant-eligible:** quadruplet **daratumumab-VRd** induction (PERSEUS: better PFS) [@perseus] → autologous HSCT → lenalidomide-based maintenance.
- **Transplant-ineligible:** Dara-Rd, or anti-CD38 + VRd.
- **Relapse:** CAR-T (cilta-cel, ide-cel) and bispecifics (BCMA, GPRC5D, FcRH5 targets).
- **Supportive:** bisphosphonate or denosumab; VTE prophylaxis with IMiDs; antiviral prophylaxis with proteasome inhibitors and anti-CD38; vaccines; renal protection (avoid NSAIDs and IV contrast where possible).

:::check
A 67-year-old has back pain, Ca 11.8 mg/dL, creatinine 2.4 mg/dL, Hb 9.6 g/dL, total protein 9.8 g/dL with albumin 3.1 g/dL. Which test is most useful next?
- [ ] Bone scan
- [x] SPEP with immunofixation and serum free light chains
- [ ] PTH level
- [ ] Peripheral flow cytometry
> Hypercalcemia, renal failure, anemia and a large protein gap strongly suggest myeloma. SPEP/IFE plus sFLC detect > 98% of cases. Bone scans miss lytic lesions; use low-dose whole-body CT or PET-CT.
:::

## AL amyloidosis and Waldenström {adult #al-amyloid refs=andromeda}

**AL amyloidosis:** misfolded light chains (λ > κ) deposit in organs.
- **Heart:** restrictive cardiomyopathy, low voltage, ↑NT-proBNP/troponin; the main cause of death.
- **Kidney:** nephrotic-range albuminuria.
- **Nerves:** peripheral and autonomic neuropathy, carpal tunnel.
- **Liver**; **macroglossia, periorbital purpura.**
- **Diagnosis:** tissue **Congo red apple-green birefringence**, then **mass-spectrometry typing** (to exclude ATTR, which is treated very differently).
- **Treatment:** **daratumumab-CyBorD** (ANDROMEDA: higher hematologic response) [@andromeda]; ASCT in selected patients; cardiac stage (Mayo) guides intensity.

**Waldenström macroglobulinemia:** IgM paraprotein, lymphoplasmacytic infiltrate, **MYD88 L265P (> 90%)**.
- **Hyperviscosity** (visual change, headache, bleeding) → **plasmapheresis**.
- Also: cold agglutinins, cryoglobulins, neuropathy (anti-MAG).
- **Treatment:** BTK inhibitors (zanubrutinib) or bendamustine-rituximab.
