## Hemolysis markers and their pitfalls {both #markers refs=nathan-oski,aiha-consensus-2020}

| Marker | Hemolysis pattern | Pitfalls |
|---|---|---|
| Reticulocytes | ↑ (ARC > 100 × 10⁹/L) | **Can be low**: parvovirus, concurrent iron/folate deficiency, autoantibody against precursors (~20% of AIHA), marrow disease |
| LDH | ↑ (very high in intravascular hemolysis and TMA) | Also ↑ in ineffective erythropoiesis (B12), tissue injury, malignancy |
| Haptoglobin | ↓ or undetectable | **Physiologically low in neonates and young infants.** Acute-phase reactant (inflammation can mask hemolysis). Congenital ahaptoglobinemia |
| Indirect bilirubin | ↑ | Gilbert syndrome; neonatal physiology |
| Urine | **Hemoglobinuria** (dipstick blood +, no RBCs on microscopy) = intravascular | Myoglobinuria gives the same dipstick result |
| DAT | + in immune hemolysis | False negatives (low-affinity IgG, IgA, few bound IgG); positive in ~1 in 10,000 healthy donors and after IVIG |

**Intravascular vs extravascular**

| | Intravascular | Extravascular (spleen/liver) |
|---|---|---|
| Features | Hemoglobinemia, hemoglobinuria, very high LDH, absent haptoglobin, hemosiderinuria (chronic) | Jaundice, splenomegaly, spherocytes, gallstones |
| Examples | **ABO transfusion reaction, PCH, G6PD crisis, PNH, TMA, mechanical valves, severe burns, malaria, clostridial sepsis** | HS, warm AIHA, SCD (mostly), thalassemia, PK deficiency |

:::check
A 4-month-old has Hb 8.1 g/dL, retic 9%, indirect bilirubin 3.5 mg/dL, and haptoglobin < 10 mg/dL. How do you interpret the low haptoglobin?
- [x] Not useful for hemolysis at this age; it is often physiologically low in young infants
- [ ] Diagnostic of intravascular hemolysis
- [ ] Indicates congenital ahaptoglobinemia
- [ ] Rules out hereditary spherocytosis
> Haptoglobin synthesis is immature in early infancy, so a low level is uninterpretable. Rely on the retic count, bilirubin, smear, LDH and DAT. The high retic and indirect hyperbilirubinemia already point to hemolysis.
:::

## A DAT-first approach to hemolysis {both #approach refs=aiha-consensus-2020}

:::algo
1. **Confirm hemolysis:** retic, LDH, bilirubin, haptoglobin (age caveat), smear, urine.
2. **DAT**
   - **IgG ± C3** → warm AIHA (drugs, SLE, CLL, immunodeficiency/Evans in children), delayed hemolytic transfusion reaction, HDFN.
   - **C3 only** → cold agglutinin disease or syndrome (*Mycoplasma*, EBV, lymphoma), **paroxysmal cold hemoglobinuria (Donath-Landsteiner)**, some drug-induced.
3. **DAT negative: let the smear lead**
   - **Spherocytes** → HS (also DAT-negative AIHA).
   - **Schistocytes** + low platelets → **TMA** (HUS, TTP, DIC) or mechanical.
   - **Bite/blister cells** → **G6PD** (Heinz bodies on supravital stain).
   - Sickle cells / targets → hemoglobinopathy.
   - Elliptocytes / pyropoikilocytes → HE/HPP.
   - Acanthocytes → liver disease (spur cells).
   - **Nothing specific** → **PK deficiency**, unstable Hb, **PNH** (flow cytometry), Wilson disease (**Coombs-negative hemolysis + liver disease in an adolescent**).
:::

## Hereditary spherocytosis {peds #hs refs=bsh-hs-2011}

- Most common inherited membrane disorder in Northern European ancestry (~1:2,000). **Autosomal dominant in ~75%.** Defects in ankyrin, spectrin, band 3 or protein 4.2. Loss of membrane surface makes the cells spheres, which the spleen removes.
- **Presentation:** neonatal jaundice (may need exchange), anemia, **splenomegaly, pigment gallstones** (even in childhood), and **aplastic crises with parvovirus**.
- **Labs:** spherocytes, **↑MCHC (> 36 g/dL)**, ↑retic, DAT negative, family history.
- **Diagnosis:** **EMA binding (flow cytometry)** is preferred; osmotic gradient ektacytometry as an alternative; osmotic fragility is outdated [@bsh-hs-2011].
- **Management**
  - Folic acid for moderate to severe disease.
  - Transfusion is often needed in the **first year** (transient). Recovery is slow.
  - **Splenectomy for severe disease** (transfusion dependence, growth failure), ideally **≥ 6 years**; **partial splenectomy** in younger children.
  - Before splenectomy: vaccinate against pneumococcus, meningococcus and Hib, and plan antibiotic prophylaxis.
  - Cholecystectomy for symptomatic stones.

:::check
A 7-year-old has intermittent jaundice, a palpable spleen, Hb 9.8 g/dL, MCHC 37.5 g/dL, retic 8%, negative DAT, and an aunt who had a splenectomy. What is the best confirmatory test?
- [ ] Osmotic fragility on fresh blood
- [x] Eosin-5-maleimide (EMA) binding by flow cytometry
- [ ] Hemoglobin electrophoresis
- [ ] Bone marrow aspirate
> This is HS until proven otherwise. EMA binding is sensitive and specific and has replaced osmotic fragility in guidelines.
:::

## G6PD deficiency {both #g6pd refs=who-g6pd-2022,aap-bili-2022}

- **X-linked.** The most common enzyme defect worldwide (~400–500 million people). Heterozygous females can be affected (lyonization), so use a **quantitative assay** in females.
- **Triggers:**
  - **Infection (most common).**
  - **Fava beans.**
  - **Drugs:** primaquine and **tafenoquine** (test before giving), dapsone, **rasburicase (contraindicated)**, **methylene blue (can cause hemolysis and fails to treat methemoglobinemia)**, nitrofurantoin, some sulfonamides, naphthalene mothballs.
- **Acute hemolysis:** abrupt pallor, jaundice, **dark urine** 1–3 days after the trigger. Bite and blister cells; Heinz bodies on supravital stain.
- **Neonatal hyperbilirubinemia** is a major cause of kernicterus worldwide. G6PD deficiency is an **AAP 2022 neurotoxicity risk factor**.
- **Testing pitfall:** reticulocytes have more enzyme, so **activity can read falsely normal during or just after hemolysis.** Repeat ~2–3 months later, or interpret against the retic count.
- **WHO 2022 classification:** class A (< 20% activity, chronic hemolysis), B (< 45%, triggered hemolysis), C (60–150%, no hemolysis), U (uncertain) [@who-g6pd-2022].

:::check
A 3-year-old boy develops dark urine and pallor 2 days after a febrile illness. Smear shows bite cells. G6PD activity drawn that day is normal. What is the next step?
- [ ] G6PD deficiency is excluded; test for PNH
- [x] Repeat G6PD activity ~2–3 months after recovery (or interpret against the retic count)
- [ ] Start prednisone for DAT-negative AIHA
- [ ] Bone marrow biopsy
> During acute hemolysis the oldest, most deficient cells are destroyed first and young reticulocytes with high G6PD activity remain. That gives a false-normal result.
:::

## Pyruvate kinase deficiency and other enzymopathies {both #pk refs=mitapivat-thal-2025}

- **PK deficiency:** autosomal recessive; the most common glycolytic enzyme defect. Chronic DAT-negative hemolysis with **no specific smear finding** (echinocytes after splenectomy).
- **↑2,3-BPG** shifts the O₂ curve right, so patients tolerate low Hb better than expected.
- **Iron overload occurs even without transfusion.**
- **Mitapivat** (oral PK activator) is approved for adults with PK deficiency. In Dec 2025 it was also approved for **adult α- and β-thalassemia**, with a hepatotoxicity REMS [@mitapivat-thal-2025].
- **Others:** pyrimidine 5′-nucleotidase deficiency (**coarse basophilic stippling**, also seen in lead poisoning); glucose phosphate isomerase deficiency.

## Immune hemolysis: warm, cold and PCH {both #aiha refs=aiha-consensus-2020}

| | Warm AIHA | Cold agglutinin disease/syndrome | **PCH** |
|---|---|---|---|
| Antibody | IgG (37 °C) | IgM, complement (C3) | **IgG biphasic anti-P (Donath-Landsteiner)** |
| DAT | IgG ± C3 | **C3 only** | **C3 only** |
| Hemolysis | Extravascular, spherocytes | Agglutination, acrocyanosis; worse in cold | **Acute intravascular**: hemoglobinuria |
| Who | [[peds]] post-viral, Evans syndrome; [[adult]] CLL, SLE, drugs, idiopathic | [[adult]] CAD (clonal B-cell); [[both]] *Mycoplasma*, EBV (transient) | [[peds]] **young children after viral infection** |
| Treat | **Steroids** (prednisone 1–2 mg/kg/day), rituximab second-line; transfuse if needed | **Keep warm**; treat infection; CAD: rituximab-based, **sutimlimab** (anti-C1s) | **Keep warm**, supportive, transfuse; self-limited |

- **Never withhold transfusion in life-threatening AIHA** because of "incompatible" crossmatches. Give the **least incompatible** units slowly with blood bank guidance.
- **Children with AIHA plus ITP (Evans syndrome)** or with lymphadenopathy should be screened for **ALPS, CVID, CTLA4/LRBA and other immune dysregulation disorders**, and SLE.

:::check
A 4-year-old develops sudden pallor and red-brown urine a week after a URI. Hb 6.2 g/dL. DAT is positive for C3 only. Which test confirms the most likely diagnosis?
- [ ] Cold agglutinin titer
- [x] Donath-Landsteiner test
- [ ] Osmotic fragility
- [ ] ADAMTS13 activity
> Acute intravascular hemolysis in a young child after a viral illness with a C3-only DAT is paroxysmal cold hemoglobinuria. The biphasic IgG anti-P binds in the cold and lyses at 37 °C. Keep the child warm; it is self-limited.
:::

## Hemolytic uremic syndrome {peds #hus refs=wong-stec-2000,ardissino-2016}

**STEC-HUS** is the most common cause of acute kidney injury in young children.
- **Course:** Shiga toxin–producing *E. coli* (O157:H7 and non-O157) causes **(bloody) diarrhea**, then **5–10 days later** MAHA, thrombocytopenia and AKI. HUS follows ~10–15% of O157 infections in children under 10.
- **Do:**
  - **Early isotonic volume expansion** during the diarrheal phase. It reduces oligoanuria and dialysis [@ardissino-2016].
  - Monitor CBC, creatinine and electrolytes daily during the high-risk window.
  - Dialysis as needed (~50%). Transfuse RBCs for symptomatic anemia.
- **Don't:**
  - **Give antibiotics for suspected STEC diarrhea** — they increase HUS risk in children [@wong-stec-2000].
  - Give antimotility agents.
  - Transfuse platelets unless bleeding or for a procedure.

**Other HUS forms to recognize**
- **Complement-mediated (atypical) HUS:** no STEC, relapsing course, family history, **anti-factor H antibodies (adolescents)**, CFH/CFI/MCP/C3/CFB variants. Treat with **eculizumab or ravulizumab**.
- **Pneumococcal HUS:** invasive pneumococcal disease (empyema, meningitis) in a child < 2 years. **DAT may be positive** (T-antigen exposure). **Avoid plasma**; use washed products per blood bank.
- **Cobalamin C defect:** an **infant** with TMA. Check **homocysteine and MMA**; treat with hydroxocobalamin.

:::check
A 3-year-old has had bloody diarrhea for 3 days. Stool is positive for Shiga toxin. Creatinine and CBC are normal. Which intervention reduces the risk of severe HUS?
- [ ] Azithromycin
- [ ] Loperamide
- [x] Isotonic IV fluids and daily labs
- [ ] Prophylactic plasma infusion
> Early volume expansion is associated with less oligoanuria and dialysis. Antibiotics and antimotility agents raise HUS risk. Monitor daily during the 5–10-day window.
:::

## Thrombotic thrombocytopenic purpura {both #ttp refs=isth-ttp-2020,hercules,plasmic,radamts13-2024}

- **Mechanism:** severe **ADAMTS13 deficiency (< 10%)** leaves ultra-large VWF multimers, which cause platelet microthrombi. The result is MAHA plus severe thrombocytopenia plus organ ischemia (brain, heart, kidney — **renal failure is usually mild**, unlike HUS).
- **The "pentad" is present in < 10% of cases.** Don't wait for it. Schistocytes plus thrombocytopenia is enough to act.
- **Immune TTP (iTTP)** [[adult]]: autoantibody; mostly adults 30–50, women. Use the **PLASMIC score** while ADAMTS13 is pending (≥ 6 = high probability) [@plasmic].
- **Treatment of iTTP** [@isth-ttp-2020]:
  - **Therapeutic plasma exchange**, **corticosteroids** and **caplacizumab** (anti-VWF; faster platelet recovery, fewer exacerbations) [@hercules].
  - Add **rituximab** for relapse prevention.
  - **Avoid platelet transfusion** unless bleeding is life-threatening.
- **Congenital TTP (Upshaw-Schulman)** [[peds]]: biallelic ADAMTS13 mutations. Presents as **severe neonatal jaundice or thrombocytopenia**, recurrent childhood episodes, or first presentation in pregnancy. Treat with plasma infusion or **recombinant ADAMTS13**, approved Nov 2023 for prophylaxis and on-demand therapy [@radamts13-2024].

## Paroxysmal nocturnal hemoglobinuria {adult #pnh}

- Acquired **PIGA** mutation in a stem cell → loss of GPI-anchored complement regulators (**CD55, CD59**) → **complement-mediated intravascular hemolysis**.
- **Triad:** hemolysis (DAT-negative, very high LDH, hemoglobinuria), **thrombosis in unusual sites** (Budd-Chiari, mesenteric, cerebral veins), and **marrow failure** (PNH clones are common in aplastic anemia).
- **Diagnosis:** **flow cytometry (FLAER, CD59)** on granulocytes and RBCs.
- **Treatment:** C5 inhibitors (eculizumab, ravulizumab). Proximal inhibitors for extravascular hemolysis on C5 blockade: pegcetacoplan (C3), **iptacopan** (oral factor B), danicopan (factor D add-on). **Meningococcal vaccination** is required.
- [[peds]] PNH is rare in children, but **screen every child with aplastic anemia for a PNH clone**.
