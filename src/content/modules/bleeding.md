## Taking a bleeding history {both #history refs=isth-bat,acog-hmb-2019}

**The history predicts bleeding better than any screening lab.** Use a validated tool:
- **ISTH-BAT** abnormal: **≥ 3 in children**, ≥ 4 in adult men, ≥ 6 in adult women [@isth-bat].
- Pediatric Bleeding Questionnaire (PBQ) for children.

**Ask about:**
- Epistaxis (> 10 min, both nostrils, needing care), bruising (> 2.5 cm, unexplained, on the trunk).
- Bleeding after dental extraction, tonsillectomy, circumcision or surgery.
- **Heavy menstrual bleeding.**
- Umbilical stump or post-vaccination bleeding (newborns).
- Joint or muscle bleeds; family history; medications (NSAIDs, SSRIs, supplements).

| Pattern | Suggests |
|---|---|
| **Mucocutaneous** (petechiae, epistaxis, gums, heavy menses) | **Platelets or VWF** |
| **Deep** (joints, muscles), delayed post-op bleeding | **Coagulation factors** (hemophilia) |
| **Umbilical stump**, ICH, poor wound healing | **FXIII** or afibrinogenemia |
| Delayed bleeding after an initially normal hemostasis | Fibrinolytic disorders (α2-antiplasmin, PAI-1), FXIII |

:::pearl Heavy menstrual bleeding in adolescents
Flooding, changing pads more than every 1–2 h, clots > 2.5 cm, periods > 7 days, or anemia/iron deficiency. **Screen for bleeding disorders, especially VWD**, if HMB began at menarche or is severe: CBC, ferritin, PT/aPTT, fibrinogen, VWF panel [@acog-hmb-2019].
:::

## Bleeding disorder or child abuse? {peds #abuse refs=aap-abuse-bleeding-2022,ten4facesp}

- **"Those who don't cruise rarely bruise."** Any bruise in a **pre-mobile infant** is a sentinel finding.
- **TEN-4-FACESp** bruising rule (children < 4 years; sensitivity ~96%) [@ten4facesp]:
  - **TEN:** Torso, Ears, Neck.
  - **FACES:** Frenulum, Angle of jaw, Cheeks (fleshy), Eyelids, Subconjunctivae.
  - **Any bruise in an infant ≤ 4.99 months.**
  - **p**atterned bruising.
  - A positive rule → **child abuse evaluation**.
- **When a bleeding disorder must be excluded** (AAP 2022) [@aap-abuse-bleeding-2022]:
  - **CBC, PT, aPTT, fibrinogen, VWF antigen and activity, FVIII, FIX.**
  - **For ICH add FXIII**, and consider broader testing with hematology.
  - Interpret with age-specific ranges. Remember that **VWF rises with stress**.
- **A bleeding disorder does not exclude abuse.** They can coexist. Severe injury rarely comes from a mild disorder.

## Interpreting PT and aPTT {both #screening-labs refs=wfh-2020,andrew-dev-hemostasis}

| PT | aPTT | Bleeds? | Think |
|---|---|---|---|
| ↑ | Normal | Yes | **FVII deficiency**, early warfarin or vitamin K deficiency, early liver disease |
| Normal | ↑ | **Yes** | **FVIII, FIX, FXI deficiency; VWD** (low FVIII); heparin |
| Normal | ↑ | **No** | **FXII, prekallikrein, HMWK deficiency; lupus anticoagulant** (thrombotic) |
| ↑ | ↑ | Yes | **Vitamin K deficiency/warfarin (severe), liver disease, DIC**, deficiency of FX, FV, FII or fibrinogen; DOACs; massive transfusion |
| Normal | Normal | Yes | **VWD (often), platelet function disorders, FXIII deficiency**, fibrinolytic defects, vascular (EDS), scurvy, mild factor deficiency |

- **Thrombin time ↑:** heparin, **dabigatran**/argatroban, low or dysfunctional fibrinogen.
- **Pre-analytic errors are common:**
  - **Heparin contamination from lines** (TT ↑, corrects with heparinase).
  - **Underfilled citrate tubes.**
  - **Hct > 55%** (too little plasma for the citrate; adjust the volume).
  - Traumatic draws.
- **Neonates:** PT and aPTT are physiologically longer. Use gestational age–specific ranges [@andrew-dev-hemostasis].

:::check
A healthy 8-year-old has an aPTT of 68 s (normal < 38) on preoperative screening. He had a tonsillectomy without bleeding. The mixing study corrects. FVIII, FIX and FXI are normal. What is the likely explanation?
- [ ] Mild hemophilia A
- [x] Factor XII deficiency
- [ ] Lupus anticoagulant
- [ ] Type 2N VWD
> A long aPTT that corrects on mixing, with normal intrinsic bleeding factors and no bleeding history, points to a contact-factor deficiency (FXII, prekallikrein, HMWK). These don't cause bleeding, and surgery can proceed. A lupus anticoagulant usually fails to correct.
:::

## Mixing studies and lupus anticoagulant {both #mixing refs=isth-la-2020}

- Mix patient plasma **1:1 with normal plasma**. **Correction means deficiency** (50% of any factor normalizes clotting times). **No correction means an inhibitor.**
- **Immediate and incubated mixes** (1–2 h at 37 °C):
  - **FVIII inhibitors are time- and temperature-dependent.** They may correct immediately and prolong after incubation.
  - **Lupus anticoagulant acts immediately.**
- **Quantify specific factor inhibitors with a Bethesda assay.**
- **Lupus anticoagulant testing:** two different tests (dRVVT plus an LA-sensitive aPTT) with screen–mix–confirm steps [@isth-la-2020]. Heparin and DOACs cause false positives.
- [[peds]] **Transient LA after viral infections is common in children.** It usually causes neither bleeding nor thrombosis. **Exception: LA–hypoprothrombinemia syndrome** (anti-prothrombin antibodies, ↓FII, prolonged PT) can cause significant bleeding.

## Von Willebrand disease {both #vwd refs=vwd-dx-2021,vwd-mx-2021}

- **The most common inherited bleeding disorder** (symptomatic ~1 in 1,000). VWF mediates platelet adhesion (GPIb) and **carries FVIII**.

**Diagnosis (ASH/ISTH/NHF/WFH 2021)** [@vwd-dx-2021]
- **Type 1:** **VWF < 0.30 IU/mL regardless of bleeding, or < 0.50 IU/mL with abnormal bleeding.**
- **VWF activity/antigen ratio < 0.7 → type 2**: confirm with multimers and specific assays.
- Prefer **newer platelet-binding activity assays (VWF:GPIbM / GPIbR)** over VWF:RCo.
- Don't use ABO-specific reference ranges. Group O levels are ~25% lower, but the diagnostic cut-offs apply.

| Type | Defect | Key features |
|---|---|---|
| **1** (~75%) | Partial quantitative deficiency | Ratio ≥ 0.7; **usually DDAVP-responsive**; type 1C = rapid clearance (↑VWFpp/Ag) |
| 2A | ↓ high-molecular-weight multimers | Ratio < 0.7 |
| **2B** | **Gain of function: ↑GPIb affinity** | **Thrombocytopenia**; enhanced low-dose ristocetin aggregation; **DDAVP can worsen thrombocytopenia** |
| 2M | ↓ platelet binding, normal multimers | Ratio < 0.7 |
| **2N** | **Can't bind FVIII** | **Low FVIII with normal VWF; mimics mild hemophilia A** but autosomal recessive (affects girls) |
| **3** | Virtually absent VWF | **Severe**, joint bleeds, FVIII ~1–10%; alloantibodies possible |

**Pitfalls:**
- VWF is an **acute-phase reactant**. It rises with **stress, a crying child, exercise, inflammation, pregnancy and estrogen**, so **repeat testing** if suspicion is high.
- Sample handling (cold storage, delays) can lower levels falsely.

**Management** [@vwd-mx-2021]
- **Desmopressin (DDAVP)** 0.3 µg/kg IV/SC, or intranasal 150 µg (< 50 kg) / 300 µg (≥ 50 kg).
  - **Do a trial first** (measure levels at 1 and 4 h).
  - **Restrict fluids for 24 h** (hyponatremia and seizures). **Avoid under age 2.**
  - Tachyphylaxis after 2–3 doses. Not effective in type 3; relatively contraindicated in 2B.
- **Tranexamic acid** for mucosal bleeding, dental work and HMB.
- **VWF concentrates** (plasma-derived VWF/FVIII or **recombinant VWF**) for non-responders, major surgery and type 3. **Long-term prophylaxis** for frequent severe bleeds.
- **Surgery targets:** VWF and FVIII ≥ 0.50 (minor) or ≥ 1.0 IU/mL (major). **Neuraxial anesthesia in pregnancy:** ≥ 0.50–1.50 IU/mL.
- **HMB:** hormonal therapy (combined hormonal contraception, **LNG-IUD**) ± TXA.
- **Acquired von Willebrand syndrome:** aortic stenosis (Heyde), **LVAD/ECMO**, **Wilms tumor** [[peds]], hypothyroidism, MGUS, ET with platelets > 1,000.

- [x] Type 1 VWD

## Hemophilia A and B {both #hemophilia refs=wfh-2020,haven-1,sippet,fitusiran}

- **X-linked** (FVIII: A, ~80–85%; FIX: B). **~1/3 have no family history** (de novo).
- **Carriers can bleed.** Measure factor levels in all potential carriers. **Women and girls with < 40%** are classified as having hemophilia [@wfh-2020].

| Severity | Factor level | Bleeding |
|---|---|---|
| **Severe** | **< 1%** | Spontaneous joint and muscle bleeds from crawling/walking age |
| Moderate | 1–5% | After minor trauma; occasionally spontaneous |
| Mild | > 5 to < 40% | With surgery or major trauma (often diagnosed late) |

**By age** [[peds]]:
- **Newborn:** ICH (avoid vacuum/forceps), cephalohematoma, **circumcision and heel-stick bleeding**.
- **Crawling/walking:** bruising, oral bleeding, **hemarthrosis** (ankles, knees, elbows; target joints → arthropathy).
- **Iliopsoas bleed:** hip held flexed, femoral nerve palsy, can hide large volumes.
- **Diagnosis:** prolonged aPTT (may be normal in mild disease), then specific factor assays. **Cord blood FVIII is reliable at birth.** FIX is physiologically low in newborns, so **mild hemophilia B needs retesting at ~6 months**.

**Modern management (WFH 2020)**
- **Prophylaxis is standard for severe disease.** Start early (before age ~3 or the second joint bleed).
- **Factor dosing (standard half-life):**
  - **FVIII: 1 IU/kg raises ~2 IU/dL** (units = kg × desired rise × 0.5).
  - **FIX: 1 IU/kg raises ~1 IU/dL** (less in children with some recombinant products).
  - **Targets:** joint bleeds 40–60 IU/dL; **life-threatening bleeds/ICH 80–100 IU/dL**.
  - Use the [factor calculator](/tools/calculators).
- **Emicizumab** (bispecific FIXa–FX bridge, SC weekly to monthly) for hemophilia A **with or without inhibitors, from birth** [@haven-1].
- **Anti-TFPI antibodies:** **concizumab** (with inhibitors 2024, without 2025) and **marstacimab** (without inhibitors, 2024), age ≥ 12.
- **Fitusiran** (antithrombin-lowering siRNA, ≥ 12 years, with or without inhibitors, every 1–2 months; dosing guided by antithrombin level; thrombosis and gallbladder/liver warnings) [@fitusiran].
- **Gene therapy** (adults): etranacogene (B) and valoctocogene (A). Uptake has been limited, and one hemophilia B product (fidanacogene) was withdrawn by its maker in 2025.
- **Inhibitors** occur in **~25–35% of previously untreated severe hemophilia A**, usually within the first 50 exposure days. Plasma-derived FVIII had a lower inhibitor rate than recombinant in SIPPET [@sippet]. Management: immune tolerance induction; bypassing agents (rFVIIa, aPCC); emicizumab.
- **Avoid NSAIDs/aspirin and IM injections.** Give vaccines SC (or IM with the smallest needle plus pressure). **Treat first, image later** for suspected head or iliopsoas bleeds.

:::pitfall Emicizumab breaks the lab
Emicizumab **normalizes the aPTT** and **invalidates one-stage FVIII and Bethesda assays**. Use **chromogenic assays with bovine reagents**. Treat breakthrough bleeds with FVIII (no inhibitor) or rFVIIa. **Avoid aPCC > 100 U/kg/24 h** (thrombotic microangiopathy and thrombosis).
:::

## Rare factor deficiencies {both #rare-factors refs=wfh-2020}

| Deficiency | Clue | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| **FXI** (hemophilia C) | ↑aPTT; **Ashkenazi Jewish ancestry**; bleeding after surgery at fibrinolytic sites (mouth, nose, urinary tract) | Levels predict bleeding poorly; **TXA**, FXI concentrate or plasma |
| **FVII** | **Isolated ↑PT** | Variable bleeding; rFVIIa |
| **FXIII** | **Normal PT/aPTT**; **umbilical stump bleeding**, ICH, poor wound healing, miscarriage | FXIII activity assay; **monthly prophylaxis** (long half-life) |
| **Afibrinogenemia / dysfibrinogenemia** | ↑PT, ↑aPTT, **↑↑TT**; umbilical bleeding. Dysfibrinogenemia: **Clauss low vs antigen normal**; bleeding **or** thrombosis | Fibrinogen concentrate |
| FV, FX, FII | ↑PT and ↑aPTT | FX: severe; plasma, PCC, specific concentrates |
| Combined FV + FVIII | Both ~5–30% | LMAN1/MCFD2 |
| α2-antiplasmin, PAI-1 | **Delayed bleeding**, normal screening labs | **Antifibrinolytics** |

## Liver disease and acquired inhibitors {both #liver-acquired refs=aga-cirrhosis-2019}

- **Liver disease means rebalanced hemostasis:** pro- and anticoagulant factors are both low, and FVIII and VWF are high. **INR doesn't predict procedural bleeding in cirrhosis.** Avoid prophylactic plasma to "correct" the INR [@aga-cirrhosis-2019]. Viscoelastic testing guides bleeding management. Cirrhosis is **prothrombotic** (portal vein thrombosis).
- **Vitamin K deficiency vs liver failure:** a vitamin K trial corrects the PT within ~12–24 h in deficiency (cholestasis, CF, biliary atresia, malnutrition, antibiotics), not in synthetic failure. **FVIII is normal or high in liver disease and low in DIC.**
- [[adult]] **Acquired hemophilia A:** autoantibody against FVIII in older adults, postpartum women, or with autoimmunity/cancer.
  - **Sudden extensive bruising or muscle bleeds with no prior history.**
  - Isolated ↑aPTT that **fails to correct after incubation**; low FVIII; Bethesda titer.
  - **Treat:** bypassing agents, recombinant porcine FVIII, or emicizumab, **plus immunosuppression** (steroids ± rituximab/cyclophosphamide).
