## Classification in 2022+: genetics first {both #classification refs=who-haem5-myeloid,icc-2022}

:::update Two classifications, one direction {year=2022}
**WHO-HAEM5** and the **ICC** (both 2022) classify myeloid neoplasms by **defining genetic abnormalities first**, morphology second [@who-haem5-myeloid] [@icc-2022].
:::

- **The "≥ 20% blasts = AML" rule no longer applies to genetically defined AML.**
  - **WHO-HAEM5:** AML with defining genetic abnormalities (e.g., PML::RARA, RUNX1::RUNX1T1, CBFB::MYH11, NPM1, KMT2A-r) needs **no blast threshold**, except BCR::ABL1 and CEBPA (≥ 20%).
  - **ICC:** ≥ 10% blasts for most recurrent-genetic AML, plus an **MDS/AML** category for 10–19% blasts.
  - Otherwise **≥ 20% blasts** in blood or marrow defines acute leukemia.
- **MDS** is now "myelodysplastic **neoplasm**". Subtypes are defined by SF3B1, del(5q), biallelic TP53, or blast counts.
- **CML:** WHO-HAEM5 drops the accelerated phase (chronic → blast phase, with high-risk features); ELN/ICC still use it.
- Gene fusions are written with a double colon: **BCR::ABL1**.
- **Auer rods = myeloid lineage** (AML, or high-grade MDS). They are **never** seen in ALL.

## Pediatric ALL {peds #all-peds refs=aall1731,who-haem5-lymphoid}

**Epidemiology:** the **most common childhood cancer** (~25%). **Peak age 2–5.** **B-ALL ~85%**, T-ALL ~15%. Risk is ~10–20× higher in Down syndrome and in cancer predisposition syndromes.

**Presentation:**
- Pallor, fever, bruising/petechiae.
- **Bone pain, limp or refusal to walk** (often **night pain**).
- Hepatosplenomegaly, lymphadenopathy.
- **T-ALL:** mediastinal mass, high WBC, adolescent boys.
- **WBC may be low, normal or high, and blasts may be absent from the blood.**

:::redflag ALL mimics: don't be falsely reassured
- **"ITP" with any other abnormality** (anemia, neutropenia, abnormal WBC, organomegaly, lymphadenopathy, bone pain) → smear review and marrow.
- **"JIA" with night pain, cytopenias or high LDH** → marrow **before any steroids**.
- **Never give corticosteroids** to a child with unexplained cytopenias or organomegaly before excluding leukemia. They can mask the diagnosis and compromise risk assignment.
:::

**Initial work-up:**
- CBC with smear; CMP with **uric acid, phosphorus, K, Ca, LDH** (tumor lysis); coagulation studies (fibrinogen); type and screen.
- **Chest X-ray before any sedation** (mediastinal mass).
- **Peripheral blood flow cytometry**; bone marrow (morphology, flow, cytogenetics/FISH, molecular).
- **Diagnostic LP with intrathecal chemotherapy** by an experienced operator (traumatic taps with blasts worsen prognosis).
- Echocardiogram before anthracyclines; **TPMT/NUDT15 genotyping** for thiopurine dosing.

**Risk stratification:**
- **NCI standard risk:** age 1–9.99 y **and** WBC < 50 × 10⁹/L. Otherwise high risk.
- **Favorable genetics:** high hyperdiploidy (51–67 chromosomes), **ETV6::RUNX1** (t(12;21)), DUX4-rearranged.
- **Unfavorable genetics:** **KMT2A-r** (especially infants), **hypodiploidy < 44**, iAMP21, TCF3::HLF, BCR::ABL1 and Ph-like (**add TKI for ABL-class**), IKZF1^plus.
- **End-of-induction MRD is the most powerful prognostic factor** and drives intensity.

**Therapy:** induction → consolidation → interim maintenance → delayed intensification → maintenance (6-MP daily, methotrexate weekly). Total ~2–2.5 years, with CNS-directed intrathecal therapy. Cranial radiation is now rarely used.

:::update Blinatumomab moves to frontline for standard-risk B-ALL {year=2025}
**COG AALL1731:** adding blinatumomab cycles to chemotherapy in NCI standard-risk B-ALL (average/high-risk features) improved **3-year DFS to 96.0% vs 87.9%** [@aall1731]. Overall survival for childhood ALL is now **~90%+**.
:::

**Key toxicities:**
- **Asparaginase:** pancreatitis, **thrombosis (including CSVT)**, hypersensitivity, hyperglycemia, hepatotoxicity.
- **Steroids:** AVN (adolescents), hyperglycemia, mood changes.
- **Vincristine:** neuropathy, constipation, jaw pain.
- **Methotrexate:** mucositis, leukoencephalopathy.
- **PJP prophylaxis (TMP-SMX); no live vaccines.**

## Adult ALL {adult #all-adult refs=who-haem5-lymphoid}

- **Outcomes are much worse than in children:** biology (more Ph+ and Ph-like disease) and tolerance.
- **AYA patients (~15–39) do better on pediatric-inspired regimens.**
- **Ph+ ALL (~25–30%, more with age):** TKI-based therapy (ponatinib or dasatinib) combined with steroids and/or **blinatumomab**, increasingly without intensive chemotherapy.
- **Ph-negative B-ALL:** adding **blinatumomab** to consolidation in MRD-negative adults improved overall survival (E1910). Inotuzumab and **CAR-T** for relapsed disease. Allo-HSCT for high-risk or MRD-positive disease.

## Pediatric AML {peds #aml-peds refs=who-haem5-myeloid,revumenib}

- **~15–20% of childhood leukemia.** Peaks in **infancy** and **adolescence**.
- **Clues more typical of AML than ALL:**
  - **Gingival hypertrophy**, skin infiltration (**"blueberry muffin" in infants**).
  - **Chloromas** (orbit, epidural).
  - **DIC** (APL, monocytic).
  - **Leukostasis at lower WBC.**
- **Favorable:** **t(8;21) RUNX1::RUNX1T1**, **inv(16) CBFB::MYH11**, **NPM1**, CEBPA bZIP, APL, **ML-DS**.
- **Unfavorable:** monosomy 7, **FLT3-ITD with high allelic ratio** (add FLT3 inhibitor), certain KMT2A partners, **NUP98::NSD1**, **CBFA2T3::GLIS2** (infant non-DS megakaryoblastic), inv(3).
- **Therapy:** intensive cytarabine/anthracycline induction ± **gemtuzumab ozogamicin** (CD33). HSCT in first remission for high-risk disease. **OS ~65–70%.**
- **Revumenib** (menin inhibitor) is FDA-approved for relapsed/refractory **KMT2A-rearranged** acute leukemia in patients **≥ 1 year** (2024) [@revumenib].

## Acute promyelocytic leukemia (APL): the treatable emergency {both #apl refs=apl0406,aaml1331,eln-aml-2022}

- **PML::RARA** (t(15;17)). Smear: hypergranular promyelocytes, **bundles of Auer rods (faggot cells)**.
- **The main threat is early hemorrhagic death from DIC with hyperfibrinolysis** (very low fibrinogen).

:::redflag Start ATRA on suspicion
**Give all-trans retinoic acid as soon as APL is suspected** from the smear and coagulopathy. **Don't wait for genetic confirmation.**
- **Support aggressively:** platelets > 30–50 × 10⁹/L, **fibrinogen > 100–150 mg/dL (cryoprecipitate)**, plasma for prolonged INR.
- Avoid LPs and central line placement until the coagulopathy is controlled.
:::

- **Low/intermediate risk (WBC ≤ 10 × 10⁹/L): ATRA + arsenic trioxide**, superior to ATRA + chemotherapy [@apl0406]. [[peds]] Children: ATRA + ATO **with little or no anthracycline**, EFS > 95% (AAML1331) [@aaml1331].
- **High risk (WBC > 10):** add an anthracycline or GO to ATRA + ATO.
- **Differentiation syndrome** (fever, dyspnea, infiltrates, effusions, edema/weight gain, hypotension, AKI) → **dexamethasone** immediately. Pause ATRA/ATO if severe; give prophylactic steroids with high WBC.
- **ATO prolongs the QTc.** Keep K⁺ > 4.0 and Mg²⁺ > 2.0; avoid other QT-prolonging drugs.

## Adult AML {adult #aml-adult refs=eln-aml-2022,viale-a}

- **Median age ~68.** Risk uses **ELN 2022** [@eln-aml-2022]:
  - **Favorable:** CBF, NPM1 without FLT3-ITD, CEBPA bZIP.
  - **Adverse:** TP53, complex/monosomal karyotype, myelodysplasia-related mutations (ASXL1, RUNX1, SRSF2, etc.).
- **Fit:** 7+3 (+ midostaurin or quizartinib for FLT3; + GO for CBF), or CPX-351 for secondary/MDS-related AML. **Allo-HSCT** in first remission for intermediate/adverse risk.
- **Unfit/older:** **azacitidine + venetoclax** (VIALE-A: median OS 14.7 vs 9.6 months) [@viale-a]. Targeted options include **IDH1/2 inhibitors** and, for relapsed disease, **gilteritinib (FLT3)** and **menin inhibitors** (KMT2A-r, NPM1).

## Chronic myeloid leukemia {both #cml refs=eln-cml-2020,asc4first}

- **BCR::ABL1.** Leukocytosis with a **myelocyte bulge, basophilia, eosinophilia**, thrombocytosis, **splenomegaly**.
- **TKIs:** imatinib, dasatinib, nilotinib, bosutinib. **Asciminib** (STAMP inhibitor) had a higher 48-week MMR than other TKIs in **newly diagnosed** CML (ASC4FIRST; FDA 2024) [@asc4first]. Ponatinib or asciminib for **T315I**.
- **Monitoring:** BCR::ABL1 (IS) every 3 months. **ELN optimal milestones: ≤ 10% at 3 months, ≤ 1% at 6 months, ≤ 0.1% (MMR) at 12 months** [@eln-cml-2020].
- **Treatment-free remission:** possible after ≥ 4–5 years of TKI with sustained deep molecular response (MR4 ≥ 2 years). About half stay in remission off therapy.
- [[peds]] **Pediatric CML** (~2–3% of childhood leukemia) presents with larger spleens and higher WBC. **TKIs slow linear growth, especially before puberty**: monitor height velocity and bone health.

## Chronic lymphocytic leukemia {adult #cll refs=iwcll-2018}

- **Diagnosis (iwCLL 2018):** **≥ 5 × 10⁹/L clonal B cells** for ≥ 3 months. **CD5+, CD19+, CD23+, dim CD20 and dim light-chain restriction.** Smudge cells are a smear artifact, not diagnostic.
- **Treat only active disease:**
  - Progressive marrow failure (Hb < 10, platelets < 100).
  - Massive or progressive splenomegaly or lymphadenopathy.
  - Lymphocyte doubling time < 6 months.
  - Refractory autoimmune cytopenias.
  - B symptoms.
  - **The lymphocyte count alone is not an indication.**
- **Prognosis:** IGHV mutation status, **del(17p)/TP53** (avoid chemotherapy), complex karyotype.
- **First-line:** continuous **BTK inhibitor** (acalabrutinib or zanubrutinib; ibrutinib has more AF/hypertension), or **fixed-duration venetoclax-based** therapy (with obinutuzumab or a BTKi). Chemoimmunotherapy (FCR) is largely obsolete.
- **Complications:**
  - **Hypogammaglobulinemia/infections** (IVIG if recurrent).
  - **Warm AIHA, ITP, pure red cell aplasia.**
  - **Richter transformation** (rapid nodal growth, ↑LDH, B symptoms → PET-guided biopsy).
  - Skin cancers.
  - Non-live vaccines only.

## MDS and JMML {both #mds-jmml refs=who-haem5-myeloid}

[[adult]] **MDS:**
- Cytopenias plus dysplasia (≥ 10% of a lineage) or defining genetics. Risk by **IPSS-M**.
- **Lower-risk anemia:** ESA, **luspatercept**, lenalidomide for **del(5q)**, imetelstat.
- **Higher-risk:** hypomethylating agents, **allo-HSCT** if eligible.
- **CHIP/CCUS:** clonal hematopoiesis without dysplasia. It carries cardiovascular and malignancy risk.

[[peds]] **Pediatric MDS** is rare and often **hypocellular** ("childhood MDS with low blasts"). **A germline predisposition is present in many**: GATA2, SAMD9/9L, Fanconi, SDS, RUNX1. Monosomy 7 → HSCT.

[[peds]] **JMML (juvenile myelomonocytic leukemia):**
- Child **< 6 years** (median ~2). **Monocytosis ≥ 1 × 10⁹/L**, splenomegaly, **↑HbF for age**, blasts < 20%.
- A **RAS-pathway** mutation (PTPN11, NRAS, KRAS, **NF1**, CBL) or monosomy 7. **GM-CSF hypersensitivity.**
- **Noonan syndrome–associated** and some **CBL** cases regress spontaneously.
- Otherwise **HSCT** is the only cure; azacitidine is used as a bridge.

## Myeloproliferative neoplasms and erythrocytosis {both #mpn refs=cyto-pv,who-haem5-myeloid}

[[adult]]
- **Polycythemia vera:**
  - Hb > 16.5 (men) / > 16.0 g/dL (women) or Hct > 49/48%, with **JAK2 V617F (~95%) or exon 12**, and **low EPO**.
  - **Phlebotomy to Hct < 45%** (CYTO-PV: fewer CV deaths and thromboses) [@cyto-pv] plus low-dose aspirin.
  - Cytoreduction (hydroxyurea, **ropeginterferon**) for high risk (age ≥ 60 or prior thrombosis).
- **Essential thrombocythemia:**
  - Platelets ≥ 450 × 10⁹/L. JAK2 (~55%), CALR (~25%), MPL (~5%), triple-negative (~10–15%).
  - **Acquired von Willebrand syndrome when platelets > 1,000** → check VWF activity before aspirin.
- **Myelofibrosis:** teardrop cells, leukoerythroblastic smear, massive splenomegaly. **JAK inhibitors** (ruxolitinib, fedratinib, pacritinib, **momelotinib** for anemia); HSCT.

[[peds]]
- **In children, thrombocytosis and erythrocytosis are almost always secondary** (infection, iron deficiency, cyanotic heart disease, hypoxia, EPO-secreting tumors).
- **Congenital erythrocytosis:**
  - **Low EPO:** EPOR mutation.
  - **Normal/high EPO:** **VHL (Chuvash)**, HIF2A/PHD2, **high-affinity Hb variants (low P50)**, 2,3-BPG mutase deficiency.

## Oncologic emergencies {both #emergencies refs=fn-peds-2023}

**Tumor lysis syndrome**
- **High risk:** Burkitt, B-ALL/T-ALL with WBC ≥ 100, AML with WBC ≥ 100, bulky disease, renal impairment.
- **Laboratory TLS (Cairo-Bishop), ≥ 2 of:**
  - Uric acid ≥ 8 mg/dL
  - K⁺ ≥ 6 mmol/L
  - **Phosphate ≥ 6.5 mg/dL in children** (≥ 4.5 in adults)
  - Ca ≤ 7 mg/dL
- **Clinical TLS:** AKI, arrhythmia, seizure.
- **Prevention:**
  - **Hyperhydration ~3 L/m²/day without potassium.**
  - **Allopurinol** (intermediate risk).
  - **Rasburicase** (high risk). **Check G6PD first**: rasburicase causes hemolysis and methemoglobinemia in G6PD deficiency.
  - No routine urine alkalinization.
  - Dialysis early for refractory hyperkalemia, hyperphosphatemia or oliguria.

**Hyperleukocytosis** (WBC > 100 × 10⁹/L)
- **Leukostasis** (hypoxemia, infiltrates, headache, confusion, retinal hemorrhage) is commoner in **AML at lower counts** than in ALL.
- **Cytoreduce promptly:** start chemotherapy or hydroxyurea.
- **Avoid or minimize RBC transfusion** (it raises viscosity). If essential, give small volumes slowly.
- Platelets and plasma for coagulopathy are fine.
- **Leukapheresis has no proven survival benefit.** Consider it selectively in symptomatic AML.

**Anterior mediastinal mass** (T-ALL/LBL, Hodgkin, PMBCL, germ cell tumor)
- **Sedation or general anesthesia can cause airway or cardiovascular collapse.** Assess orthopnea and stridor; CT for tracheal compression; echo for pericardial effusion/SVC.
- **Get the diagnosis by the least invasive route:** peripheral blood flow cytometry, pleural fluid, peripheral node under local anesthesia, or marrow.
- If the patient is unstable, start steroids or radiation even though they may compromise the diagnosis.

**Other emergencies**
- **SVC syndrome.**
- **Spinal cord compression:** back pain plus neurologic signs → dexamethasone and urgent MRI.
- **Typhlitis:** RLQ pain with neutropenia → bowel rest and anaerobic coverage.
- **Hypercalcemia:** myeloma, lymphoma.
