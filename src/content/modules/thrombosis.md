## Pediatric VTE: who gets it and why {peds #peds-vte-epi refs=ash-peds-vte-2025,kids-dott}

- **Bimodal: neonates and adolescents.** Incidence is rising with survival of complex illness and CVC use. **> 90% of children with VTE have ≥ 1 risk factor.**
- **Central venous access devices are the single biggest risk factor** (most neonatal and much childhood VTE).
- **Other risk factors:**
  - Infection (osteomyelitis, mastoiditis → sinovenous thrombosis).
  - Cancer, especially **ALL with asparaginase and steroids**.
  - Congenital heart disease (Fontan); nephrotic syndrome (antithrombin loss); IBD; SLE/APS.
  - Immobility, trauma, surgery; obesity.
  - **Estrogen-containing contraception** (adolescents).
  - Anatomic: **May-Thurner** (left iliac), **Paget-Schroetter / thoracic outlet** (effort thrombosis of the upper limb).
- **PE is under-recognized in adolescents.** Think of it with pleuritic pain, tachycardia or hypoxemia, especially with estrogen, obesity or recent surgery.
- **Inherited thrombophilia** contributes but **rarely changes acute management** (see below).

## Pediatric VTE treatment: ASH/ISTH 2025 {peds #peds-vte-treat refs=ash-peds-vte-2025,kids-dott,einstein-jr,diversity}

:::update DOACs and shorter courses {year=2025}
The 2025 ASH/ISTH update [@ash-peds-vte-2025]:
- **Suggests DOACs (rivaroxaban or dabigatran) over standard of care** (LMWH, UFH, VKA, fondaparinux) for pediatric VTE.
- **Suggests 6 weeks rather than 3 months** of anticoagulation for **select low-risk provoked VTE** (based on Kids-DOTT) [@kids-dott].
:::

**Treat or not**
- **Symptomatic DVT/PE: anticoagulate** (conditional).
- **Clinically unsuspected (incidental) DVT/PE:** either anticoagulation or none.
- **Cerebral sinovenous thrombosis**, with or without hemorrhage from venous congestion: anticoagulate; **anticoagulation alone over thrombolysis**.
- **Proximal DVT, or PE without hemodynamic compromise:** anticoagulation alone (no thrombolysis). **PE with hemodynamic compromise:** thrombolysis, then anticoagulation.
- **Superficial vein thrombosis:** cannula-related in the upper limb → no anticoagulation. Non-cannula-related, lower limb, or with cancer/varicose veins → anticoagulate.
- **CVAD-related thrombosis** in a device that's non-functioning or no longer needed: immediate or delayed removal (either). A functioning, needed CVAD can stay in place.

**Duration**

| Situation | Suggested duration |
|---|---|
| **Select low-risk provoked VTE** | **6 weeks** (continue to 3 months if occlusive thrombus persists on 6-week imaging) |
| Provoked VTE that doesn't qualify as low-risk* | **3 months** |
| **Unprovoked DVT/PE** | **6–12 months** (rather than indefinite) |

*Excluded from the 6-week course: PE, recurrent VTE, persistent occlusive thrombus at 6 weeks, cancer-associated thrombosis, persistent antiphospholipid antibodies or major thrombophilia, ongoing risk factors.

**Choosing the agent**
- **Rivaroxaban** (EINSTEIN-Jr; weight-based; suspension for small children) [@einstein-jr] or **dabigatran** (DIVERSITY; age/weight-based) [@diversity]. Both are approved for children.
- **Avoid DOACs or use with great caution:**
  - GI absorption problems.
  - Recent surgery.
  - Liver disease; GFR < 30 mL/min.
  - **Antiphospholipid syndrome.**
  - **Preterm neonates.**
  - **Active cancer.**
- **LMWH (enoxaparin):** 1.5 mg/kg q12h (< 2 months; often higher in neonates), **1 mg/kg q12h (≥ 2 months)**. Target **anti-Xa 0.5–1.0 U/mL 4–6 h post-dose.**
- **UFH** when rapid reversal may be needed (surgery, high bleeding risk).

## Adult VTE essentials {adult #adult-vte refs=ash-vte-2020,adjust-pe,traps}

- **Diagnosis:** pretest probability (Wells, YEARS) → D-dimer if low/intermediate. **Age-adjusted D-dimer (age × 10 µg/L FEU) over 50** safely reduces imaging [@adjust-pe]. Then compression US or CTPA.
- **Treatment:** **DOACs over VKA** for most (ASH 2020) [@ash-vte-2020].
  - **Cancer:** apixaban, edoxaban, rivaroxaban or LMWH. Caution with GI/GU tumors.
  - **Pregnancy:** LMWH only. **Severe renal failure / mechanical valves:** not DOACs.
- **Duration:**
  - **3 months** for VTE provoked by a major transient risk factor.
  - **Unprovoked or with a persistent risk factor: indefinite** is usually suggested, with **reduced-dose apixaban or rivaroxaban** for extended therapy and periodic bleeding-risk review.
- **Subsegmental PE** with no DVT and low risk may be observed. **IVC filters** only when anticoagulation is absolutely contraindicated.
- **High-risk PE (shock):** systemic thrombolysis. Intermediate-high risk: monitor; rescue reperfusion if decompensating (PERT).

## Thrombophilia testing: when it helps {both #thrombophilia refs=ash-thrombophilia-2023}

| Thrombophilia | Approx. VTE risk | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Factor V Leiden (heterozygous) | ~3–5× | Most common in European ancestry |
| Prothrombin G20210A | ~2–3× | |
| **Antithrombin deficiency** | High | **Heparin resistance**; nephrotic syndrome causes acquired loss |
| **Protein C / S deficiency** | High | **Warfarin skin necrosis**; **homozygous → neonatal purpura fulminans** |
| **Antiphospholipid antibodies** | High (arterial and venous) | Changes the drug choice (avoid DOACs if high-risk) |

- **Testing rarely changes the treatment of a first provoked VTE.** ASH 2023 suggests testing in selected scenarios: VTE with minor or non-surgical provoking factors, **pregnancy- or estrogen-associated VTE**, strong family history (especially known high-risk thrombophilia) [@ash-thrombophilia-2023].
- [[peds]] Test in **purpura fulminans** (protein C/S), neonatal VTE without a catheter, CSVT/arterial events (± lipoprotein(a), APS), and adolescents with **unprovoked VTE**.
- **Timing pitfalls:**
  - Don't measure **antithrombin, protein C or protein S** during acute thrombosis, **on anticoagulants** (warfarin lowers C/S; heparin lowers AT), in pregnancy (low S), or in **neonates** (physiologically low).
  - DOACs interfere with clot-based assays (LA, APC resistance).
  - **Genetic tests (FVL, PT) can be done anytime.**

## Antiphospholipid syndrome {both #aps refs=aps-2023,traps,isth-la-2020}

- **Labs:** **lupus anticoagulant**, anticardiolipin and/or anti-β2-glycoprotein I (IgG/IgM, moderate–high titer), **persistent on 2 occasions ≥ 12 weeks apart**.
- **2023 ACR/EULAR classification criteria** use weighted clinical domains (venous, arterial, microvascular, obstetric, cardiac valve, thrombocytopenia) and laboratory domains. They are for research classification; clinical diagnosis is broader [@aps-2023].
- **Treatment:** **warfarin, INR 2–3**. **Avoid DOACs in triple-positive or arterial APS**: rivaroxaban caused excess arterial events in TRAPS [@traps]. Catastrophic APS: anticoagulation plus steroids plus plasma exchange/IVIG.
- [[peds]] Pediatric APS is rare. Look for SLE. Transient post-infectious antibodies are common and usually benign.

## Estrogen and VTE in adolescents {peds #estrogen refs=us-mec-2024}

- **Combined hormonal contraception raises VTE risk ~3–5×**, highest in the first months. Obesity, smoking, immobility and thrombophilia add to it.
- **US MEC 2024:** estrogen is **category 4** (unacceptable risk) with **acute DVT/PE, history of VTE at higher risk of recurrence, or known thrombogenic mutations** [@us-mec-2024].
- **Safer options:** **levonorgestrel IUD**, etonogestrel implant, progestin-only pills. All also help heavy menstrual bleeding.
- Teens on anticoagulants still need contraception: warfarin is teratogenic, and **anticoagulation worsens HMB**.

## Anticoagulant reversal {both #reversal refs=annexa-i,wfh-2020}

| Agent | Monitoring | Reversal for major bleeding |
|---|---|---|
| **UFH** | aPTT or anti-Xa (0.35–0.7) | **Protamine** 1 mg per 100 U given in the last 2–3 h (max 50 mg) |
| **LMWH** | Anti-Xa (treatment 0.5–1.0) | Protamine (partial ~60%): 1 mg per 1 mg enoxaparin if < 8 h |
| **Warfarin** | INR 2–3 | **Vitamin K IV + 4-factor PCC**; plasma only if PCC unavailable |
| **Dabigatran** | TT (sensitive), dilute TT | **Idarucizumab 5 g**; dialyzable |
| **Rivaroxaban / apixaban** | Drug-calibrated anti-Xa | **Andexanet alfa** (ANNEXA-I: better hemostasis in ICH but more thrombotic events) [@annexa-i] or **4F-PCC ~50 U/kg** |
| Argatroban / bivalirudin | aPTT | Stop (short half-life); supportive |

- [[peds]] Pediatric reversal doses are weight-based; data are limited, so involve hematology early. **Infant formula is vitamin K–rich**, and **breastfed infants are more sensitive to warfarin**, which makes VKAs hard to manage in infancy.
