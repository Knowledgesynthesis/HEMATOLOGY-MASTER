## SCD genotypes and the newborn screen {peds #genetics-nbs refs=nhlbi-scd-2014}

- **HbS:** β-globin Glu→Val (β6, HGVS p.Glu7Val). Deoxy-HbS polymerizes, causing rigid sickled cells, **vaso-occlusion plus hemolysis**, and endothelial injury.
- **Sickle cell disease (SCD)** = any genotype with HbS that causes disease. Approximate severity: **HbSS ≈ HbS/β⁰-thal > HbSC ≈ HbS/β⁺-thal**. HbSD-Punjab and HbSO-Arab also sickle.
- **Newborn screening (universal in the US)** reports hemoglobins **in order of quantity**: F first, then A.

| Result | Meaning | Action |
|---|---|---|
| **FA** | Normal | — |
| **FAS** | Sickle cell **trait** (A > S) | Inform family; genetic counseling |
| **FS** | **HbSS, HbS/β⁰-thal**, or HbS/HPFH | **SCD presumed**: confirm, start **penicillin by 2 months**, refer to SCD center |
| **FSA** | **HbS/β⁺-thal** (S > A) | SCD: same as above |
| **FSC** | **HbSC disease** | SCD: same as above |
| FAC / FC | HbC trait / HbCC or HbC/β⁰ | Trait: counsel. FC: hematology follow-up |
| FAE / FE | HbE trait / **HbEE or HbE/β⁰-thal** | FE needs DNA or family studies; E/β⁰ can be transfusion-dependent |
| **FA + Barts** | α-thalassemia (more Barts → more deletions) | Follow-up; counsel; HbH possible |
| **F only** | **β⁰-thal major**, HPFH, or extreme prematurity | Repeat and confirm; urgent hematology |

:::pitfall
A **transfusion before the screen** invalidates it (donor HbA). Repeat ~3–4 months after the last transfusion, or use DNA testing.
:::

## Fever in SCD: a 60-minute emergency {peds #fever refs=nhlbi-scd-2014,props}

- **Functional asplenia begins in infancy.** Invasive pneumococcal disease risk was ~30–100× higher before prophylaxis.
- **T ≥ 38.5 °C (101.3 °F) needs immediate evaluation:**
  - History and exam; **CBC with differential, reticulocytes, blood culture**; urine culture, CXR or LP if indicated.
  - **Parenteral ceftriaxone within 60 minutes of arrival.** Don't wait for labs.
  - **Admit** if ill-appearing, < 6 months (varies by center), T ≥ 40 °C, WBC > 30 or < 5 × 10⁹/L, platelets < 100, Hb < 5 or ≥ 2 g/dL below baseline, signs of ACS, sequestration or other complications, or prior invasive infection.
- **Prophylaxis:**
  - **Penicillin VK 125 mg BID from ≤ 2 months to age 3, then 250 mg BID to age 5** (PROPS: 84% reduction in pneumococcal infection) [@props]. Continue past 5 after invasive pneumococcal disease or splenectomy.
  - **Vaccines:** complete pneumococcal conjugate series (add PPSV23 if PCV20 wasn't used); **MenACWY from infancy, MenB from 10 years**; annual influenza; COVID-19 per schedule.
  - **Malaria prophylaxis for travel** (asplenia is a risk).

## Vaso-occlusive pain {both #voc refs=ash-scd-pain-2020,nhlbi-scd-2014,bellet-1995}

**ASH 2020 and NHLBI 2014 essentials** [@ash-scd-pain-2020]
- **Analgesia within 60 minutes of ED arrival** (30 min of triage). Reassess every 30–60 min. Use the patient's **individualized pain plan**.
- **Opioids** (IV/SC; PCA when admitted) **plus scheduled NSAIDs** if renal function allows. Avoid meperidine.
- **Subanesthetic ketamine infusion** is an adjunct for refractory pain. Regional anesthesia can help selected patients.
- **Euvolemia, not boluses:** oral fluids or IV at maintenance unless dehydrated. Over-hydration can precipitate ACS.
- **O₂ only for hypoxemia** (SpO₂ < 95% on room air).
- **Incentive spirometry every 2 h while awake** reduces ACS in chest/back pain [@bellet-1995].
- Look for triggers and complications: fever, ACS, sequestration, priapism, AVN, osteomyelitis (*Salmonella*, *S. aureus*).
- **Dactylitis** (painful swollen hands/feet) is often the **first manifestation at 6–24 months**.

:::pitfall
Pain is real even when vital signs and labs are "normal". **No lab test confirms or excludes a VOC.** Under-treatment and delays are the most documented failures in SCD care.
:::

## Acute chest syndrome {both #acs refs=nacssg,nhlbi-scd-2014,ash-scd-tx-2020}

- **Definition:** **new pulmonary infiltrate** plus fever and/or respiratory symptoms (cough, tachypnea, chest pain, hypoxemia).
- The **leading cause of death and ICU admission** in SCD. Often begins **24–72 h into a hospitalization for pain**.
- **Causes:** infection (*Chlamydia*, *Mycoplasma*, viruses), fat embolism from infarcted marrow, hypoventilation [@nacssg].
- **Treat:**
  - **IV cephalosporin plus a macrolide**; O₂ to SpO₂ ≥ 95%; incentive spirometry; bronchodilators if wheezing; careful analgesia and fluids.
  - **Simple transfusion** if Hb is > 1 g/dL below baseline (don't exceed ~10–11 g/dL).
  - **Exchange transfusion** for severe or progressive disease: SpO₂ < 90% despite O₂, worsening distress or infiltrates, or falling Hb despite simple transfusion.

## Acute splenic sequestration {peds #sequestration refs=nhlbi-scd-2014}

- **Definition:** **Hb drop ≥ 2 g/dL below baseline plus an acutely enlarging spleen**, with ↑retic and often ↓platelets. It can progress to **hypovolemic shock within hours**.
- **Peak age:** **6 months to 5 years in HbSS** (before autoinfarction). In **HbSC and HbS/β⁺ it can occur in older children and adults.**
- **Treat:** fluids and **cautious transfusion in small aliquots** (e.g., 5 mL/kg). The spleen releases trapped cells as it shrinks, so aim below baseline to avoid **hyperviscosity overshoot**.
- **Recurrence ~50%.** Options: chronic transfusion until age ≥ 2, then **splenectomy**. **Teach caregivers to palpate the spleen.**

## Aplastic crisis {peds #aplastic-crisis refs=nhlbi-scd-2014}

- **Parvovirus B19** infects erythroid progenitors and halts RBC production for ~7–10 days. With RBC survival of only ~10–20 days, Hb falls fast.
- **Clues:** pallor and fatigue **without** splenomegaly; **retic < 1%** (vs ↑retic in sequestration).
- **Management:** transfusion; **droplet isolation** (pregnant staff and other patients with hemolytic disease are at risk). **Check siblings with SCD.** HS and other chronic hemolytic states get the same crisis.

## Stroke prevention and acute stroke {peds #stroke refs=ash-scd-cns-2020,stop,twitch,ohene-frempong-1998}

- Without screening, **~11% of children with HbSS have an overt stroke by age 20** [@ohene-frempong-1998].
- **Transcranial Doppler (TCD) yearly from 2 to 16 years** (HbSS, HbS/β⁰).
  - **TAMMV ≥ 200 cm/s (non-imaging) = abnormal** → **chronic transfusion** keeping HbS < 30% (STOP: **~90% relative risk reduction**) [@stop].
  - 170–199 = conditional → repeat sooner.
- **TWiTCH:** after **≥ 1 year of transfusion** with **no severe vasculopathy**, **hydroxyurea at MTD** can replace transfusion for primary prevention [@twitch].
- **Silent cerebral infarcts** are common and cause neurocognitive harm. ASH suggests **screening MRI once in early school age** (and once in adults) [@ash-scd-cns-2020].
- **Acute neurologic deficit (stroke/TIA):**
  - **Immediate transfusion**, within 2 h of presentation. **Exchange preferred**; give simple transfusion first if exchange will be delayed and Hb permits.
  - Then **chronic transfusion** for secondary prevention.
  - Image with MRI/MRA, but **don't delay transfusion for imaging**.

## Priapism and other acute complications {both #priapism refs=nhlbi-scd-2014}

- **Priapism > 4 h is a urologic emergency:** aspiration and irrigation (± phenylephrine). **Exchange transfusion is not first-line.** Stuttering episodes: hydration, analgesia, urinate, warm shower. Discuss prevention.
- **Acute anemia differential:** sequestration (↑retic, big spleen), aplastic crisis (↓retic), **delayed hemolytic transfusion reaction / hyperhemolysis** (recent transfusion), hyperhemolysis with infection.
- **Acute abdominal pain:** VOC vs cholecystitis, hepatic sequestration, mesenteric ischemia, constipation (opioids).
- **Acute vision loss** (hyphema, retinal artery occlusion) and **acute hepatic crises** are rarer emergencies.

## Hydroxyurea: the backbone {peds #hydroxyurea refs=nhlbi-scd-2014,baby-hug,ash-hu-draft}

- **Offer to all infants ≥ 9 months with HbSS or HbS/β⁰, regardless of severity** (NHLBI 2014; BABY HUG) [@baby-hug]. Also for adults with ≥ 3 pain crises/year, ACS, or severe anemia.
- **Effects:** ↑HbF, ↓neutrophils and adhesion, larger MCV. Results: **fewer pain crises and ACS episodes, fewer transfusions and admissions, lower mortality**, preserved spleen and kidney function in young children, and can replace transfusion for TCD (TWiTCH).
- **Dosing:**
  - **Start 20 mg/kg/day** (children; adults 15 mg/kg/day).
  - **Escalate by 5 mg/kg every 8 weeks to MTD** (max 35 mg/kg/day), keeping **ANC ≥ 2.0 × 10⁹/L** (≥ 1.25 in young children) and **platelets ≥ 80 × 10⁹/L**.
  - CBC/retic every 4 weeks during escalation, then every 2–3 months.
  - Track adherence by MCV and HbF trends.
- **Counsel:** contraception; it is traditionally held in pregnancy and breastfeeding. **It is not leukemogenic in SCD in long-term follow-up.**

:::update ASH hydroxyurea dosing guideline in progress {year=2026}
Draft ASH recommendations (public comment closed Jan 2026) favor **algorithm- or PK-guided starting doses** and **dose escalation over fixed dosing**. They are not final yet [@ash-hu-draft].
:::

## Transfusion in SCD {both #transfusion-scd refs=ash-scd-tx-2020,taps}

- **Phenotype-match for Rh (C, E) and K** for all patients; extended matching if alloimmunized [@ash-scd-tx-2020]. Get an **RBC antigen genotype** before the first transfusion if possible.
- **Before surgery** with general anesthesia (HbSS/HbS/β⁰): **transfuse to Hb ~10 g/dL** (TAPS reduced complications) [@taps].
- **Avoid post-transfusion Hb > 10–11 g/dL** with simple transfusion (hyperviscosity) unless doing exchange.
- **Chronic transfusion** (stroke, abnormal TCD, recurrent ACS): target pre-transfusion HbS < 30%. Monitor iron (ferritin, liver MRI) and chelate.

:::redflag Delayed hemolytic transfusion reaction / hyperhemolysis
**5–15 days after a transfusion:** new pain, dark urine, and **Hb below the pre-transfusion level** with falling HbA%. Donor **and** recipient cells are being destroyed. **Avoid further transfusion if possible.** Give IVIG plus high-dose steroids; consider eculizumab. Alert the blood bank and record it permanently.
:::

## Cure and disease modification: 2026 landscape {both #curative refs=casgevy-peds-2026,exacel-2024,lovocel-2022,voxelotor-withdrawal}

| Option | Status | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| **Hydroxyurea** | Standard for all | See above |
| **Matched sibling HSCT** | Curative; **EFS ~90%+ in children** | Best results young, before organ damage; indications include stroke and recurrent ACS/pain |
| **Exa-cel (Casgevy)**: CRISPR edit of the *BCL11A* enhancer | FDA 2023 ≥ 12 y; **expanded to ≥ 2 years (July 2026)** for SCD with recurrent VOCs and TDT [@casgevy-peds-2026] | 29/30 (97%) free of VOCs ≥ 12 months [@exacel-2024]; myeloablative busulfan (infertility → fertility preservation) |
| **Lovo-cel (Lyfgenia)**: lentiviral anti-sickling β-globin | FDA 2023 ≥ 12 y | **Boxed warning: hematologic malignancy**; lifelong monitoring [@lovocel-2022] |
| L-glutamine | FDA ≥ 5 y (2017) | Modest reduction in crises; not approved in EU |
| Crizanlizumab (anti-P-selectin) | FDA-approved; **phase 3 STAND showed no reduction in crises**; EU authorization revoked | Use is now limited |
| **Voxelotor** | **Withdrawn worldwide Sept 2024** (more VOCs and deaths in post-marketing studies) [@voxelotor-withdrawal] | Patients were transitioned off it |

:::update Two practice-changing updates {year=2026}
**Voxelotor is gone (2024).** **Gene editing (exa-cel) now reaches children from age 2 in the US (2026).** Eligibility, fertility preservation and access are now routine discussions in pediatric SCD clinics.
:::

## Chronic complications and screening {both #chronic refs=nhlbi-scd-2014,ash-scd-cns-2020}

| System | Complication | Screen / act |
|---|---|---|
| CNS | Stroke, silent infarcts, neurocognitive deficits | TCD 2–16 y (HbSS/Sβ⁰); MRI once at school age; school support |
| Kidney | **Hyposthenuria (enuresis)**, albuminuria, CKD | **Urine albumin yearly from age 10**; ACE inhibitor if persistent |
| Eye | Proliferative retinopathy (**HbSC > HbSS**) | **Dilated retinal exam from age 10**, every 1–2 years |
| Bone | AVN (hip/shoulder), osteomyelitis | Imaging for persistent pain/limp |
| Lung | Pulmonary hypertension, asthma, sleep apnea | Symptom-driven echo (TRV ≥ 2.5 m/s raises risk) |
| Gallbladder | Pigment stones | Cholecystectomy if symptomatic |
| Growth | Delayed growth and puberty | Growth charts; endocrine evaluation |
| Iron | Overload with chronic transfusion | Ferritin, liver MRI R2*/T2* |

## Sickle cell trait {both #trait refs=nhlbi-scd-2014}

- HbAS: **HbS ~35–40%** (lower if α-thal coexists). **Normal blood counts and life expectancy.**
- **Real risks:**
  - Hematuria (papillary necrosis), hyposthenuria.
  - **Renal medullary carcinoma** (young people with trait or SCD presenting with **hematuria/flank pain** → urgent imaging).
  - **Exertional rhabdomyolysis and heat illness** (use universal precautions: hydration, acclimatization, rest).
  - Splenic infarction at altitude.
  - Modest ↑VTE risk.
- **Genetic counseling:** two carriers have a **25% chance per pregnancy** of a child with SCD.

## β-Thalassemia {both #beta-thal refs=tif-2021,mitapivat-thal-2025,casgevy-peds-2026}

- **Spectrum:** trait (minor) → **non-transfusion-dependent (NTDT, "intermedia")** → **transfusion-dependent (TDT, "major")**. Classification is now by transfusion need (TIF).
- **TDT presentation:** pallor, FTT, hepatosplenomegaly at **6–24 months** as HbF falls. Smear: microcytosis, targets, nRBCs, teardrops. HPLC: **↑↑HbF, little or no HbA**.
- **Transfusion:** every 2–5 weeks, keeping **pre-transfusion Hb 9.5–10.5 g/dL** (higher with cardiac disease). This suppresses ineffective erythropoiesis, bone deformity and extramedullary hematopoiesis [@tif-2021].
- **Iron chelation:**
  - Start after ~10–20 transfusions or **ferritin > 1,000 ng/mL**.
  - Agents: deferasirox (oral; monitor renal and liver function), deferoxamine (SC infusion), deferiprone (oral; **weekly ANC for agranulocytosis**).
  - Monitor: **liver iron (MRI R2/R2*)** and **cardiac T2*** (< 20 ms = cardiac iron; < 10 ms = high risk), yearly from ~age 10.
- **Complications:** cardiomyopathy and arrhythmia (the leading cause of death), endocrinopathies (hypogonadism, diabetes, hypothyroidism), osteoporosis, infection (*Yersinia* with deferoxamine), thrombosis after splenectomy.
- **NTDT** is not benign: iron overload **without** transfusion (↓hepcidin), extramedullary masses, thrombosis, pulmonary hypertension, leg ulcers.
- **Curative and disease-modifying options:**
  - Matched sibling HSCT in childhood (OS > 90%).
  - Gene therapy: beti-cel; **exa-cel (US ≥ 2 years as of 2026)**.
  - **Luspatercept** (adults; ↓transfusion burden).
  - **Mitapivat** (adults with α- or β-thal, FDA Dec 2025; liver REMS) [@mitapivat-thal-2025].

## α-Thalassemia {both #alpha-thal refs=tif-2021}

There are four α-globin genes (two on each chromosome 16). Severity follows the number of nonfunctional genes.

| Genotype | Name | Phenotype |
|---|---|---|
| −α/αα | Silent carrier | Normal; low Hb Barts at birth |
| **−−/αα (cis)** or **−α/−α (trans)** | α-thal trait | Mild microcytosis; normal adult HPLC |
| −−/−α | **HbH disease** | Hb ~7–10, hemolysis, splenomegaly, **HbH inclusions ("golf balls")**; oxidant-triggered crises. **HbH–Constant Spring** is more severe |
| −−/−− | **Hb Barts hydrops fetalis** | Fetal hydrops. Historically fatal; **in-utero transfusions now allow survival** (lifelong transfusion or HSCT) |

- **cis deletions (−−/αα)** are common in Southeast Asian and Mediterranean ancestry. Two cis carriers have a **25% risk of Hb Barts hydrops** per pregnancy, so offer prenatal diagnosis. The **trans (−α/−α)** configuration, common in African ancestry, cannot produce hydrops offspring with another trans carrier.
- **Maternal risks with a hydropic fetus:** preeclampsia, mirror syndrome, hemorrhage.

## Hb electrophoresis / HPLC interpretation {both #hplc refs=tif-2021}

| Pattern (adult/older child) | Interpretation |
|---|---|
| HbA ~97%, A₂ 2–3.5%, F < 1% | Normal |
| A ~60%, S ~40% | Sickle trait (S < 35% suggests coexisting α-thal) |
| S 80–95%, F 2–20%, **no A**, A₂ normal | HbSS (untransfused) |
| S > A, ↑A₂ | **HbS/β⁺-thal** |
| S only + ↑A₂ (> 3.5%), microcytosis | **HbS/β⁰-thal** (vs HbSS + α-thal: needs DNA testing) |
| S ~50%, C ~50% | HbSC |
| **A₂ > 3.5%** ± mildly ↑F, microcytosis | **β-thal trait** |
| **↑↑F, little/no A** | β-thal major / TDT |
| A₂ "> 10%" | Likely **HbE** (co-elutes with A₂ on many HPLC systems) |

**Caveats:**
- Transfusion within ~3 months invalidates results.
- **Iron deficiency lowers A₂.**
- HbF is high in infancy, HPFH, hydroxyurea therapy and some marrow disorders.
- α-thal trait is invisible on adult HPLC.
