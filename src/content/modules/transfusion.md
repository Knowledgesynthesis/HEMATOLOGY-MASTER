## Blood products at a glance {both #products refs=bsh-neonatal-2016}

| Product | Contains | Storage | Typical effect |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Red cells** | RBCs (Hct ~55–65% in additive solution) | 1–6 °C, 35–42 days | Adult 1 unit ≈ **+1 g/dL**. Child **10–15 mL/kg ≈ +2–3 g/dL** |
| **Platelets** (apheresis or pooled) | Platelets in plasma | **20–24 °C, agitated**, 5–7 days (bacterial risk) | Adult 1 unit ≈ +30–50 × 10⁹/L. Child **10–15 mL/kg ≈ +50–100** |
| **Plasma** (FFP/PF24) | All coagulation factors | Frozen ≤ −18 °C | **10–15 mL/kg raises factors ~15–20%** |
| **Cryoprecipitate** | **Fibrinogen**, FVIII, VWF, **FXIII**, fibronectin | Frozen | **1 unit per 5–10 kg ≈ +50–100 mg/dL** fibrinogen (adult pool of 10) |
| Low-titer O whole blood | All components | 1–6 °C | Trauma resuscitation (adults, increasingly children) |
| Granulocytes | Neutrophils | Use within 24 h, irradiated | Selected severe neutropenic infections |

**Product modifications**

| Modification | Purpose | Indications |
|---|---|---|
| **Leukoreduction** | ↓FNHTR, ↓HLA alloimmunization, **↓CMV transmission ("CMV-safe")** | Universal in many systems |
| **Irradiation** | **Prevents TA-GVHD** | **Intrauterine and neonatal exchange transfusions**; **suspected or known congenital T-cell immunodeficiency (SCID, 22q11)**; HSCT; **Hodgkin lymphoma**; purine analogues (fludarabine), ATG/alemtuzumab; **directed donations from relatives**; HLA-matched platelets; granulocytes |
| **Washing** | Removes plasma and K⁺ | Severe or recurrent allergic reactions; **IgA deficiency with anti-IgA**; hyperkalemia risk (large-volume neonatal) |
| Pathogen reduction (platelets) | ↓Bacterial and viral risk | Can replace irradiation and CMV-safe status |

:::pearl Neonates and potassium
Stored and **irradiated** red cells leak K⁺. For **large-volume neonatal transfusion** (exchange, ECMO prime, cardiac surgery, massive transfusion), use **fresh units (< 5–7 days)**, irradiated < 24 h before use, or washed, to prevent hyperkalemic arrest.
:::

## Compatibility and pre-transfusion testing {both #compatibility refs=bsh-neonatal-2016}

| Recipient | RBCs from | Plasma from |
|---|---|---|
| O | O | O, A, B, **AB** |
| A | A, O | A, AB |
| B | B, O | B, AB |
| AB | AB, A, B, O | AB |

- **O RBCs = universal red cell donor. AB plasma = universal plasma donor.** Group A plasma with low-titer anti-B is an accepted alternative in trauma.
- **RhD:** give **D-negative RBCs to D-negative girls and women of childbearing potential**. In emergencies, D-positive units are acceptable for males and older women.
- **Type and screen:** ABO/D plus antibody screen. **Crossmatch:** electronic (if screen negative and two concordant types) or serologic (~45 min). An antibody-positive patient needs antigen-negative units, which can take hours. **Send early.**
- [[peds]] **Infants < 4 months** don't make ABO antibodies, but **maternal IgG** may be present. Testing is simplified: forward type plus a screen on mother or infant. **Repeat crossmatches aren't needed during the admission** if there are no unexpected antibodies. Use O (or ABO-compatible with both mother and infant) RBCs.
- [[both]] **SCD and thalassemia:** extended phenotype or genotype; **Rh (C, E) and K matching** (see [SCD transfusion](/learn/hemoglobinopathies#transfusion-scd)).

## Red cell transfusion thresholds {both #rbc-thresholds refs=aabb-rbc-2023,mint,tripicu,top,ettno}

**Restrictive thresholds are safe for most patients** [@aabb-rbc-2023]

| Population | Threshold (Hb, g/dL) | Evidence |
|---|---|---|
| [[adult]] Hospitalized, hemodynamically stable | **7** | AABB 2023 (strong) |
| [[adult]] Cardiac surgery | 7.5 | AABB 2023 |
| [[adult]] Orthopedic surgery or pre-existing CVD | 8 | AABB 2023 |
| [[adult]] **Acute MI with anemia** | Individualize; many use < 8–10 | MINT: liberal (< 10) vs restrictive (7–8), primary outcome not significant (16.9% vs 14.5%, P = 0.07), trend favoring liberal [@mint] |
| [[peds]] **Critically ill, stable children** | **7** | TRIPICU, AABB 2023 [@tripicu] |
| [[peds]] **Congenital heart disease** | **7 after biventricular repair; 9 for single-ventricle palliation** | AABB 2023 |
| [[peds]] **Preterm infants** | TOP restrictive thresholds (week 1: 10–11; week ≥ 3: 7–8.5) | TOP, ETTNO [@top] [@ettno] |

**Thresholds don't apply to:** active hemorrhage, symptomatic anemia (transfuse for physiology, not a number), SCD (specific indications), chronic transfusion programs (thalassemia, SCD), or ACS without judgment.

**One unit at a time in adults:** reassess after each unit.

## Platelet transfusion thresholds (AABB/ICTMG 2025) {both #platelet-thresholds refs=aabb-plt-2025,planet-2,patch}

| Situation (no major bleeding) | Transfuse below (× 10⁹/L) |
|---|---|
| Hypoproliferative (chemotherapy, allo-HSCT), adults and children | **10** |
| Autologous HSCT; chronic stable aplastic anemia (adults) | **No prophylaxis** |
| Consumptive thrombocytopenia (adults) | 10; **dengue: no prophylaxis** |
| **Preterm neonates** | **25** (PlaNeT-2) [@planet-2] |
| CVC placement at a compressible site | 10 |
| **Lumbar puncture** | **20** |
| Interventional radiology: low / high risk | 20 / 50 |
| Major non-neuraxial surgery | 50 |
| Neurosurgery / ocular (conventional) | ~100 |

- **Don't transfuse** for non-operative ICH with platelets > 100. **Don't transfuse for antiplatelet-associated ICH**; PATCH showed harm [@patch].
- **Avoid platelets in ITP, TTP and HIT** except for life-threatening bleeding.
- **Refractoriness:** 1-h corrected count increment < 5,000–7,500. Non-immune causes (fever, sepsis, DIC, splenomegaly, drugs) vs **HLA alloimmunization** → HLA-matched or crossmatched platelets.

## Plasma and cryoprecipitate: indications and misuses {both #plasma-cryo refs=aga-cirrhosis-2019}

**Plasma**
- **Use for:** bleeding with multiple factor deficiencies (DIC, liver disease, massive transfusion), **plasma exchange for TTP**, a specific deficiency with no concentrate (FV, FXI in some regions).
- **Don't use for:**
  - Volume expansion.
  - **Prophylactic INR "correction" before procedures** in non-bleeding patients (INR < 2 doesn't predict bleeding).
  - **Warfarin reversal when 4F-PCC is available.**

**Cryoprecipitate**
- **Use for:** **fibrinogen < 150 mg/dL with bleeding** (< 200 in obstetric hemorrhage), DIC, massive transfusion, APL. Fibrinogen concentrate is an alternative.
- **Not for hemophilia A, VWD or FXIII deficiency when concentrates are available.**

## Pediatric dosing and practicalities {peds #peds-dosing refs=bsh-neonatal-2016}

- **Red cells:** volume (mL) = **ΔHb (g/dL) × weight (kg) × 3–5** (factor ~4 for Hct ~60% units). **10–15 mL/kg ≈ +2–3 g/dL.**
  - Rate ~5 mL/kg/h; finish each unit within 4 h.
  - **Severe chronic anemia (Hb < 5) or heart failure:** small aliquots (e.g., 5 mL/kg over 3–4 h), consider diuretics, or partial exchange.
- **Platelets:** 10–15 mL/kg (neonates 15 mL/kg). Avoid large volumes of ABO-incompatible plasma in small children.
- **Plasma:** 10–15 mL/kg. **Cryoprecipitate:** 1 unit per 5–10 kg.
- **Neonatal double-volume exchange:** **2 × blood volume** (term ~85 mL/kg; preterm ~100 mL/kg). Removes ~85–90% of antibody-coated cells and ~50% of intravascular bilirubin.
  - **Use** reconstituted whole blood (fresh, irradiated, Hct ~0.5).
  - **Monitor** glucose, Ca²⁺, K⁺ and platelets.

The [Bedside Calculators](/tools/calculators) compute all of these.

## Transfusion reactions {both #reactions refs=trali-2019,taco-2018,kennedy-premed-2008}

:::algo Any suspected reaction
1. **Stop the transfusion.** Keep the IV open with saline.
2. **Check patient ID against the unit at the bedside** (clerical error causes most ABO deaths).
3. Vital signs, examination, oxygen; treat the emergency.
4. **Notify the blood bank.** Return the bag. Send a post-transfusion sample (repeat type, **DAT**, hemolysis labs, urinalysis) ± cultures (bag and patient) ± CXR/BNP.
:::

| Reaction | Timing | Key features | Management |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Acute hemolytic** (ABO) | Minutes to 24 h | Fever, chills, **flank/back pain**, hypotension, **hemoglobinuria**, DIC, AKI. Under anesthesia: hypotension and oozing | Stop; fluids to urine output > 1 mL/kg/h; treat DIC; **DAT positive** |
| **FNHTR** | During or < 4 h | **T ≥ 38 °C and rise ≥ 1 °C**, chills; otherwise well | Exclude hemolysis and sepsis; antipyretic. **Premedication doesn't prevent reactions** [@kennedy-premed-2008] |
| **Allergic** (mild) | During | **Urticaria/pruritus only** | Antihistamine; **may resume the same unit** if it resolves |
| **Anaphylactic** | Seconds to minutes | Hypotension, wheeze, angioedema, **no fever** | **Epinephrine IM**; stop permanently. Check **IgA deficiency/anti-IgA** (and haptoglobin deficiency); washed products |
| **TRALI** | **≤ 6 h** | **Hypoxemia + bilateral infiltrates**, often fever and **hypotension**; **no left atrial hypertension** | **Supportive (ventilation); avoid diuretics.** Report so the **donor is deferred** (anti-HLA/HNA) [@trali-2019] |
| **TACO** | **≤ 12 h** | Dyspnea, **hypertension**, tachycardia, JVD, edema, **↑BNP**, positive fluid balance | **Diuretics, upright, O₂; slow future transfusions** [@taco-2018] |
| **Septic** | During or < 2 h (**platelets** most often) | **T ≥ 39 °C or rise ≥ 2 °C, rigors**, hypotension | **Culture patient and bag**; broad-spectrum antibiotics; quarantine co-components |
| **Delayed hemolytic / serologic** | 24 h – 28 days | Falling Hb, jaundice, **new positive DAT and alloantibody** (classically Kidd) | Supportive; record the antibody. **In SCD, suspect hyperhemolysis** |
| **TA-GVHD** | 2–30 days | Fever, rash, diarrhea, liver dysfunction, **pancytopenia (marrow aplasia)**; > 90% fatal | **Prevention (irradiation)**; HSCT is the only rescue |
| **Post-transfusion purpura** | 5–12 days | Severe thrombocytopenia, usually in **HPA-1a-negative, previously pregnant women** | **IVIG** |
| Hypotensive | During | Isolated hypotension, often with **ACE inhibitors** | Stop; it resolves quickly |
| Metabolic | Large volume | **Hypocalcemia (citrate), hyperkalemia, hypothermia** | Calcium, fresh units, warmers |

:::check
Forty minutes into a platelet transfusion, a 68-year-old with AML becomes hypoxemic (SpO₂ 84%) with bilateral infiltrates, BP 88/50, T 38.4 °C, flat neck veins and normal BNP. What is the most likely diagnosis?
- [ ] TACO
- [x] TRALI
- [ ] Septic transfusion reaction
- [ ] Anaphylaxis
> Acute hypoxemic lung injury within 6 h, with hypotension and fever and no evidence of volume overload, is TRALI. Give respiratory support and avoid diuretics. Report to the blood supplier for donor investigation. A septic reaction is a key differential with platelets, so culture the bag and patient.
:::

:::check
A 3-year-old on a red cell transfusion develops hives without respiratory or hemodynamic changes. They resolve 15 minutes after diphenhydramine. What is next?
- [ ] Discard the unit and send a hemolysis work-up
- [x] Resume the same unit cautiously
- [ ] Give epinephrine and stop permanently
- [ ] Switch to washed products for all future transfusions
> Isolated mild urticaria is the one reaction where restarting the same unit is acceptable once symptoms resolve. Fever, hypotension, dyspnea or angioedema require stopping and a full evaluation.
:::

## Massive transfusion {both #massive refs=proppr,crash-2}

- **Activation:** [[adult]] anticipated ≥ 10 units in 24 h, or ≥ 4 units in 1 h. [[peds]] Pediatric: **> 40 mL/kg of blood products** in 24 h, or ongoing hemorrhagic shock.
- **Balanced ratio ~1:1:1 (plasma : platelets : RBC).** PROPPR showed more hemostasis and fewer exsanguination deaths at 24 h, with no difference in 30-day mortality [@proppr]. Pediatric packs are weight-scaled (e.g., 20 mL/kg RBC + 20 mL/kg plasma + 10–20 mL/kg platelets).
- **Tranexamic acid within 3 h of injury:**
  - [[adult]] 1 g over 10 min, then 1 g over 8 h [@crash-2].
  - [[peds]] 15 mg/kg (max 1 g), then 2 mg/kg/h.
- **Prevent the lethal triad:**
  - Warm all fluids.
  - **Give calcium** (citrate binds it; ionized Ca²⁺ falls fast).
  - Correct acidosis.
  - Guide with viscoelastic testing (TEG/ROTEM) and frequent labs.
  - **Cryoprecipitate or fibrinogen concentrate** early (fibrinogen falls first).
