## Neutropenia: a structured approach {peds #neutropenia refs=duffy-null-2023,harriet-lane}

- **ANC** = WBC × (% segmented + % bands). Severity (≥ 1 year): **mild 1.0–1.5, moderate 0.5–1.0, severe < 0.5 × 10⁹/L**. Infants 2–12 months normally run lower (neutropenia < 1.0).
- **Infection risk tracks the ANC, marrow reserve and the cause.** Isolated chronic neutropenia with normal marrow (autoimmune, Duffy-null) rarely causes serious infection.

| Cause | Clues |
|---|---|
| **Transient post-viral** (most common) | Recent viral illness; recovers within 2–4 weeks |
| **Duffy-null–associated neutrophil count (DANC)** | People of African/Middle Eastern ancestry; ANC often 1.0–1.5; **no infection risk**; replaces the term "benign ethnic neutropenia" [@duffy-null-2023] |
| Drug-induced | Antibiotics (β-lactams, TMP-SMX), anticonvulsants, antithyroid drugs, clozapine |
| **Autoimmune neutropenia of infancy** | 6–36 months; often ANC < 0.5 but **few serious infections**; anti-neutrophil antibodies; **resolves by ~2–3 years** |
| Neonatal alloimmune | Maternal anti-HNA; resolves in weeks |
| **Severe congenital neutropenia** (ELANE, HAX1…) | ANC < 0.5 from birth, **omphalitis, deep abscesses, oral ulcers**; G-CSF responsive; **MDS/AML risk** |
| **Cyclic neutropenia** (ELANE) | **~21-day cycles** of fever, ulcers, adenitis. **CBC 2–3×/week for 6 weeks** |
| **Shwachman-Diamond** (SBDS) | Neutropenia + **exocrine pancreatic insufficiency** (steatorrhea, FTT) + skeletal dysplasia; MDS/AML risk |
| Others | GATA2 deficiency, WHIM, Chédiak-Higashi (giant granules), GSD Ib, B12/folate/copper deficiency, hypersplenism, marrow infiltration |

:::algo
1. Repeat the CBC with smear. Check whether other lines are low (if so, think marrow).
2. Ask about severe or recurrent infections, ulcers, abscesses and family history.
3. Isolated, well child, infections not severe → recheck in 2–4 weeks (post-viral) or test **anti-neutrophil antibodies** (infant) or **Duffy phenotype** (ancestry).
4. Persistent ANC < 0.5, serious infections, or other lineages involved → **marrow exam and genetic panel**; hematology referral.
:::

> Severe isolated neutropenia in a thriving infant with only mild infections is classically autoimmune neutropenia of infancy. Antibodies cause peripheral destruction while marrow reserve is preserved. It usually resolves by age 2–3. SCN presents with severe bacterial infections from early infancy.

## Fever and neutropenia in cancer {both #febrile-neutropenia refs=fn-peds-2023}

**Pediatric guideline 2023 update** [@fn-peds-2023]
- **Definition:** fever with ANC < 0.5 × 10⁹/L, or expected to fall below that.
- **Empiric monotherapy with an antipseudomonal β-lactam** (cefepime, piperacillin-tazobactam, or a carbapenem) **within 60 minutes**. Blood cultures from every CVC lumen.
- **Add** a second gram-negative agent or a glycopeptide **only** for clinical instability, suspected resistance, or a specific indication (e.g., catheter infection, MRSA).
- **Low-risk patients** can step down to outpatient or oral therapy. Empiric antibiotics can be **stopped in clinically well patients with negative cultures who have been afebrile ≥ 24 h**, even before count recovery (criteria vary by risk group).
- **Persistent fever ≥ 96 h in high-risk patients** → evaluate for invasive fungal infection (chest CT ± other imaging) and add empiric antifungal therapy.

[[adult]] Adults: risk-stratify with **MASCC**; low-risk can be managed outpatient with oral fluoroquinolone plus amoxicillin-clavulanate.

:::redflag
**Fever + neutropenia + hypotension** is septic shock until proven otherwise. Give fluid resuscitation and broad-spectrum antibiotics (add vancomycin and an aminoglycoside) immediately, before imaging.
:::

## Aplastic anemia {both #aplastic-anemia refs=race,groarke-2022}

- **Definition (Camitta):** marrow cellularity < 25% plus ≥ 2 of ANC < 0.5, platelets < 20, or absolute retic < 60 × 10⁹/L.
  - **Severe (SAA):** ANC < 0.5.
  - **Very severe:** ANC < 0.2.
- **Causes:** idiopathic/immune (most), **seronegative hepatitis-associated** (often adolescents, weeks after hepatitis), drugs, toxins (benzene), PNH. **Always exclude inherited marrow failure before therapy** (see below).
- **Treatment**
  - [[peds]] **Matched sibling HSCT** first-line for SAA. **Upfront matched unrelated donor HSCT** is increasingly used in experienced centers. Otherwise **horse ATG + cyclosporine**. **Adding eltrombopag did not improve response in children** [@groarke-2022].
  - [[adult]] Horse ATG + cyclosporine **+ eltrombopag** (higher complete response, RACE) [@race]; HSCT for younger fit patients with a matched donor.
- **Supportive care:** irradiated, leukoreduced products; **avoid transfusions from family members** if HSCT is possible (alloimmunization); antifungal prophylaxis in profound neutropenia; iron chelation.

## Inherited bone marrow failure syndromes {peds #inherited-bmf refs=nathan-oski}

| Syndrome | Key features | Test |
|---|---|---|
| **Fanconi anemia** | Short stature, **radial ray/thumb anomalies**, café-au-lait spots, renal anomalies; macrocytosis and ↑HbF before cytopenias (median ~7 y); **AML/MDS and head-and-neck/anogenital SCC** | **Chromosome breakage (DEB/MMC)**, gene panel |
| **Diamond-Blackfan anemia** | **Red cell aplasia < 1 year** (~90%), macrocytosis, ↑HbF, **↑eADA**, craniofacial/**triphalangeal thumb** anomalies; ribosomal genes (RPS19…) | eADA, gene panel |
| Shwachman-Diamond | Neutropenia + pancreatic insufficiency + skeletal dysplasia | SBDS gene, fecal elastase |
| Dyskeratosis congenita / telomere biology disorders | Nail dystrophy, oral leukoplakia, reticulated pigmentation; pulmonary fibrosis, liver disease | **Telomere length (flow-FISH)**, panel |
| CAMT | Severe neonatal thrombocytopenia → aplasia | MPL gene |
| GATA2 deficiency | **Monocytopenia**, B/NK lymphopenia, mycobacterial/HPV infections, lymphedema, MDS | GATA2 |
| SAMD9/9L | Monosomy 7, MDS, multisystem disease | Gene panel |

**TEC vs DBA**

| | TEC | DBA |
|---|---|---|
| Age | **1–4 years** | **< 1 year** |
| MCV | Normal for age | **↑** |
| HbF / eADA | Normal | **↑ / ↑** |
| Anomalies | None | Often (thumb, craniofacial, heart, kidney) |
| Course | **Spontaneous recovery in 1–2 months** | Lifelong: steroids (after ~1 year of age), transfusions, HSCT |

:::pearl
Before treating any child for "acquired" aplastic anemia, or choosing an HSCT donor, test for **Fanconi anemia and telomere disorders**. An affected sibling donor, or full-intensity conditioning, can be catastrophic.
:::

## Reactive lymphocytosis and normal pediatric differentials {both #lymphocytosis refs=harriet-lane,iwcll-2018}

- [[peds]] **Lymphocytes normally outnumber neutrophils from ~1 week to ~4–6 years.** Don't call this lymphocytosis.
- **EBV infectious mononucleosis:** atypical (reactive, "Downey") lymphocytes, pharyngitis, posterior cervical nodes, splenomegaly. **Heterophile tests are often negative under age 4** → use EBV VCA IgM. Avoid contact sports for ~3 weeks (splenic rupture).
- **Pertussis:** marked **mature lymphocytosis**. In young infants, **WBC > 30 × 10⁹/L predicts pulmonary hypertension and death** → ICU-level care; consider leukoreduction.
- Others: CMV, acute HIV, toxoplasmosis, drug reaction (DRESS), post-splenectomy.
- [[adult]] Persistent clonal B-cell lymphocytosis: **< 5 × 10⁹/L clonal B cells without nodes or organomegaly = monoclonal B-cell lymphocytosis**; **≥ 5 × 10⁹/L = CLL** [@iwcll-2018].

## Eosinophilia {both #eosinophilia}

- **Definitions:** AEC > 0.5 × 10⁹/L (eosinophilia); **> 1.5 × 10⁹/L = hypereosinophilia**. Organ damage risk: heart (endomyocardial fibrosis), lungs, skin, nerves.
- [[peds]] **Atopy (most common in high-income settings), parasites** (*Toxocara*, *Strongyloides*, hookworm), drugs (**DRESS**), eosinophilic GI disease, immunodeficiency (hyper-IgE, Omenn).
- [[adult]] Add **myeloid/lymphoid neoplasms with eosinophilia** (*FIP1L1::PDGFRA* — **imatinib-responsive**), lymphocytic-variant HES, EGPA, adrenal insufficiency.

:::pitfall
**Test for (or empirically treat) *Strongyloides* before giving steroids** to anyone with possible exposure. Steroids can trigger fatal hyperinfection.
:::

## Leukocytosis, leukemoid reactions and leukocyte adhesion defects {both #leukocytosis refs=eln-cml-2020}

- **Neutrophilia:** infection, stress, steroids (demargination; no left shift), asplenia, smoking, sickle cell disease, G-CSF.
- **Leukemoid reaction:** WBC > 50 × 10⁹/L reactive, with left shift and toxic granulation. Causes: severe infection, *C. difficile*, systemic JIA, burns, hemolysis, and **TAM in Down syndrome** (neonates).
- **vs CML:** **basophilia**, eosinophilia, myelocyte "bulge", splenomegaly → **BCR::ABL1 by PCR/FISH**. The LAP score is obsolete.
- [[peds]] **Leukocyte adhesion deficiency:** **delayed umbilical cord separation (> 3–4 weeks)**, omphalitis, infections **without pus**, persistent marked neutrophilia even between infections.

## Hemophagocytic lymphohistiocytosis (HLH) {peds #hlh refs=hlh-2004,shlh-2025,hscore,allen-ferritin-2008}

- Uncontrolled activation of cytotoxic lymphocytes and macrophages causes a cytokine storm.
- **Primary/familial** (infants and young children): PRF1, UNC13D, STX11, STXBP2; XLP (SH2D1A, XIAP); Griscelli 2; Chédiak-Higashi.
- **Secondary:** EBV (most common trigger), other infections, malignancy (lymphoma), and **macrophage activation syndrome in systemic JIA/SLE**.
- **HLH-2004 criteria (5 of 8, or a molecular diagnosis)** [@hlh-2004]:
  1. Fever ≥ 38.5 °C
  2. Splenomegaly
  3. Cytopenias in ≥ 2 lines (Hb < 9 [< 10 under 4 weeks], platelets < 100, ANC < 1.0)
  4. Triglycerides ≥ 265 mg/dL (fasting) and/or fibrinogen ≤ 150 mg/dL
  5. Hemophagocytosis (marrow, spleen, node, liver)
  6. Low or absent NK-cell activity
  7. **Ferritin ≥ 500 µg/L**
  8. **Soluble CD25 (sIL-2R) ≥ 2,400 U/mL**
- **Ferritin > 10,000 µg/L is ~90% sensitive and ~96% specific in children** [@allen-ferritin-2008].
- **In secondary HLH, 4 of 8 criteria or an HScore ≥ 169** performed best in a 2025 multicenter validation [@shlh-2025] [@hscore].
- **Treatment:**
  - Treat the trigger.
  - **Dexamethasone ± etoposide** (HLH-94 backbone). Emapalumab (anti-IFN-γ) for refractory primary HLH; ruxolitinib is emerging.
  - **HSCT for familial disease.**
  - MAS: high-dose steroids ± **anakinra**.

## Langerhans cell histiocytosis {peds #lch}

- A **myeloid neoplasm** driven by the MAPK pathway (**BRAF V600E** in ~50–60%, MAP2K1). Peak incidence 1–4 years.
- **Sites:**
  - **Lytic skull lesions** ("punched out").
  - Seborrhea-like or eroded skin rash (infants).
  - **Central diabetes insipidus** (pituitary stalk).
  - Lung (adult smokers).
  - "**Risk organs**" (liver, spleen, marrow), which carry a worse prognosis.
- **Treatment:** single bone lesion → curettage. Multisystem → vinblastine + prednisone. Refractory or risk-organ disease → **BRAF or MEK inhibitors**. Watch for DI and late neurodegeneration.
